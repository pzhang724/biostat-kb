# Protocol Amendment

A formal change to a trial protocol that was already approved (方案修订). Amendments happen because **you cannot foresee everything at the start** — a trial is an experiment run under **uncertainty, often over years**, and learning as data come in is the whole point, so the protocol is a **living document** (动态文件). Especially in the **early phase**, the dose / schedule / population are being learned on purpose as you go, so amendments are **expected, not a failure of planning**.

## Common triggers

- **New safety information** — new AEs / [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#SUSAR — the reportable subset|SUSARs]] → dose modification, extra monitoring, new exclusion criteria, new stopping rules.
- **New efficacy / external data** — new data from this trial or other trials, or a newly approved drug, changes the standard of care (标准治疗), the dose, or the comparator (对照).
- **Recruitment problems** (招募问题) — eligibility too strict / enrollment too slow → widen inclusion-exclusion, add sites or countries.
- **Requests from a regulator or IRB/IEC** — feedback from the agency or ethics committee that requires changes.
- **Operations / feasibility** (可行性) — the [[Schedule of Assessments|assessment schedule]] is too much of a burden, changes to vendor/lab/logistics, outside disruptions (e.g. a pandemic, 大流行).
- **Design adaptations** (设计调整) — sample-size re-estimation (样本量重估), adding a cohort/arm or a dose expansion, refining endpoints / the SAP (with care).
- **Errors / clarifications** — fix inconsistencies or unclear parts (ambiguities, 歧义) in the original protocol.

## Substantial vs non-substantial

- **Substantial** (实质性; significant) — affects subject safety/rights, the scientific value, or how the trial is run → must be **re-approved by the [[IRB and IEC (Ethics Committees)|IRB/IEC]] AND the regulatory authority BEFORE it is put into practice**.
- **Non-substantial / administrative** (非实质性/行政性) — minor (an admin contact, a typo) → **notification only**.
- **Exception** — a change to remove an **immediate hazard** (紧急危险) to subjects may be put into practice **first**, then reported quickly.

A trial-conduct concept used across [[Oncology]].
