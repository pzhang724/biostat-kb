# Follow-up of Adverse Events

Once an AE is recorded, the investigator must **follow it** until there is a **final outcome** — resolution / return to baseline, stabilization (稳定), death, or until it is explained well enough or the patient is lost to follow-up (失访). Follow-up is the process that moves the [[Adverse Event Outcome|outcome]] from RECOVERING → RECOVERED and fills in the end date `AEENDTC` (and the AE duration used in analysis).

## Key points

- **It goes beyond the scheduled visits / end of treatment** if the AE is still ongoing — especially for [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)|SAEs]] and drug-related AEs. A **related SAE is followed even after** the standard [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#Safety reporting period|safety reporting period]] ends — causality (因果关系), not the calendar, decides.
- **SAE follow-up reports** — an SAE is reported in stages: an **initial report**, then **follow-up reports** as new information comes in (outcome updates, causality reassessed, extra data). The sponsor sends these on to the authorities — see the [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#Reporting flow to the regulatory authority|reporting flow]].
- **AEs still ongoing at the end of the study** — the protocol says to follow them until resolution / stabilization or a set cutoff; sometimes care is handed over to the patient's regular doctor.
- **In practice** — abnormal labs are repeated until they become normal or stable, and this is what closes `AEENDTC`.

## Not the "safety follow-up visit"

Don't confuse it with the scheduled **safety follow-up visit / period** on the [[Schedule of Assessments]] — that is a fixed study timepoint. AE follow-up is tracking of one AE at the **event level**, and it can go on past that visit. How the AE was first found is [[Adverse Event Ascertainment]]; a safety concept used across [[Oncology]].
