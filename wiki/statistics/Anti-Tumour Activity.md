# Anti-Tumour Activity

Evidence that a treatment **acts against the cancer** (抗肿瘤活性) — it shrinks tumours, slows/halts growth, or otherwise reduces tumour burden. It is the **efficacy signal**, and is the language used especially in **early-phase (Phase I/II)** trials for preliminary / exploratory efficacy, before confirmatory endpoints.

**How it's shown:** objective response (tumour shrinkage by [[RECIST 1.1]] → ORR, [[Best Overall Response]]), depth / duration of response, disease control, [[PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)|PSA]] decline in prostate cancer, PFS.

**Activity vs clinical benefit** — a key distinction: *activity* is a weaker claim (the drug does something to the tumour); *clinical benefit* (e.g. OS, symptoms / quality of life) means it actually helps the patient live longer / better. A drug can show anti-tumour activity without yet proving clinical benefit — cf. [[Surrogate Endpoint]].

**Examples of what might count as anti-tumour activity:**

1. Sum of target-lesion diameters shrinks ≥30% → a partial response (RECIST PR); lesions vanish entirely = CR.
2. 40% of patients in a cohort achieve CR/PR → ORR 40%.
3. Prostate cancer: PSA falls ≥50% from baseline (PSA50).
4. Imaging shows reduced tumour burden — no new lesions on bone scan, lower tracer uptake on [[PSMA PET-CT]].
5. Responses last longer / progression occurs later than expected (PFS gain); a waterfall plot showing most patients have some tumour shrinkage.

Common thread: all are direct signs the **tumour itself is pushed back** (shrinkage, marker decline, control) — not yet proof the patient lives longer (clinical benefit / OS).

A cross-cutting efficacy concept in [[Oncology]].
