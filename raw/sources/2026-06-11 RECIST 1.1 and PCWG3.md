Q: 说一下 RECIST 1.1 / PCWG3 Criteria

A:

RECIST 1.1 (Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors, v1.1, 2009)
- Standardized imaging-based rules for assessing solid-tumor response. The common language for ORR/PFS in solid-tumor trials.
- Measurable lesion: longest diameter >=10 mm on CT/MRI (lymph node by SHORT axis >=15 mm).
- Target lesions: up to 5 total, max 2 per organ; record the SUM OF DIAMETERS (SoD) — nodes contribute short axis. Everything else = non-target (qualitative).
- Response categories (overall, integrating target + non-target + new lesions):
  - CR: all target lesions disappear; any nodes <10 mm short axis.
  - PR: >=30% decrease in SoD vs baseline.
  - PD: >=20% increase in SoD vs the smallest-so-far (nadir) AND >=5 mm absolute increase; OR any new lesion; OR unequivocal non-target progression.
  - SD: neither PR nor PD.
- CR/PR usually require CONFIRMATION (repeat scan >=4 wks) in ORR-primary / non-randomized trials.
- Endpoints derived: ORR, DoR, PFS.

PCWG3 (Prostate Cancer Working Group 3, 2016; updates PCWG2 2008)
- Consensus design/endpoint criteria for advanced (mCRPC) prostate-cancer trials, because RECIST alone can't handle bone-predominant, non-measurable disease.
- Bone (bone scan / technetium): "2+2 rule" — progression = >=2 new lesions, then CONFIRMED by >=2 ADDITIONAL new lesions on the next scan. Designed to filter out early bone-scan "flare" (transient apparent worsening that's actually response).
- Soft tissue / nodes: assessed by RECIST 1.1.
- PSA: report PSA kinetics (waterfall of max decline). PSA progression = >=25% increase AND >=2 ng/mL above the nadir, confirmed >=3 weeks later. But de-emphasizes PSA-alone decisions.
- Core philosophy shift: separate the QUESTION "is the patient still benefiting?" from "first sign of change". Encourages treating through early PSA/scan changes and reporting progression by mode (bone vs soft tissue vs PSA vs symptoms) as SEPARATE endpoints, not one lumped PD.
- Preferred efficacy endpoints: rPFS (radiographic progression-free survival) and OS; also captures CTCs, symptomatic skeletal events, etc.
- Flare awareness: both bone-scan flare and PSA flare can mimic progression early.
