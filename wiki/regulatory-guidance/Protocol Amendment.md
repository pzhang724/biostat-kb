# Protocol Amendment

A formal change to an approved trial protocol (方案修订). They happen because **you can't foresee everything up front** — a trial is an experiment run under **uncertainty over (often) years**, and learning as data accrue is the whole point, so the protocol is a **living document**. Especially in **early phase**, dose / schedule / population are deliberately being learned as you go, so amendments are **expected, not a planning failure**.

## Common triggers

- **New safety information** — emerging AEs / [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#SUSAR — the reportable subset|SUSARs]] → dose modification, added monitoring, new exclusion criteria, new stopping rules.
- **Emerging efficacy / external data** — new data from this or other trials, or a newly approved drug, shifts standard of care, dose, or comparator.
- **Recruitment problems** — eligibility too strict / enrollment too slow → broaden inclusion-exclusion, add sites or countries.
- **Regulator or IRB/IEC requests** — agency or ethics feedback requiring changes.
- **Operational / feasibility** — [[Schedule of Assessments|assessment schedule]] too burdensome, vendor/lab/logistics changes, external disruptions (e.g. a pandemic).
- **Design adaptations** — sample-size re-estimation, adding a cohort/arm or dose-expansion, refining endpoints / the SAP (carefully).
- **Errors / clarifications** — fix inconsistencies or ambiguities in the original protocol.

## Substantial vs non-substantial

- **Substantial** (significant) — affects subject safety/rights, the scientific value, or trial conduct → must be **re-approved by [[IRB and IEC (Ethics Committees)|IRB/IEC]] AND the regulatory authority BEFORE implementation**.
- **Non-substantial / administrative** — minor (admin contact, typo) → **notification only**.
- **Exception** — a change to eliminate an **immediate hazard** to subjects may be implemented **first**, then reported promptly.
