Q: Record the SAE (Serious Adverse Event) criteria.

A: An AE is "serious" (SAE) if it meets **any one** of the regulatory seriousness criteria (ICH E2A / 21 CFR 312.32):

1. Results in **death**.
2. Is **life-threatening** — patient was at risk of death *at the time of the event* (not a hypothetical "could have been worse").
3. Requires **inpatient hospitalization or prolongation** of existing hospitalization.
4. Results in **persistent or significant disability / incapacity**.
5. Is a **congenital anomaly / birth defect**.
6. **Important medical event (IME)** — a medically important condition that may jeopardize the patient and may require intervention to prevent one of the above outcomes (the catch-all).

Key point: **serious ≠ severe.** Seriousness is a regulatory/outcome category (these 6 criteria); severity is intensity (CTCAE grade). A CTCAE grade 3 event can be non-serious, and a grade 1 event can be an SAE if e.g. it causes hospitalization.

SAE classification drives **expedited / SUSAR reporting** and is captured separately from the CTCAE grade and from the causality assessment.
