Non-hematologic toxicity DLT:
- Grade 3 or higher, with exceptions noted (elsewhere in protocol)
- Hy's law cases, defined as:
  - AST or ALT > 3x upper limit of normal (ULN), AND
  - total bilirubin > 2x ULN, AND
  - alkaline phosphatase < 2x ULN, AND
  - no other reason for liver injury

Explanation given:
Hy's law is a lab pattern flagging dangerous drug-induced liver injury (DILI). The four pieces:
1. ALT or AST >3x ULN → hepatocellular injury (肝细胞损伤): liver cells damaged, enzymes leak out.
2. Total bilirubin >2x ULN → liver's excretory/processing function failing (jaundice 黄疸); not just enzyme leak but real functional impairment.
3. ALP (alkaline phosphatase 碱性磷酸酶) <2x ULN → rules out cholestasis/biliary obstruction (胆汁淤积) as the cause of the high bilirubin → confirms hepatocellular, not a blocked duct.
4. No other cause (viral hepatitis, alcohol, other hepatotoxic drugs, etc.) → so the trial drug is the culprit.

Why it matters: the combo "real hepatocyte damage + liver failing to clear bilirubin + not explained by blockage or anything else" predicts severe/fatal DILI (historically high mortality/transplant). Even a couple of Hy's law cases in a program is a major red flag; hence it counts automatically as a DLT. (Named after Hyman Zimmerman.)
