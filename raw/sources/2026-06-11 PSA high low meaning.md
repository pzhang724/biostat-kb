Q: PSA 高低意味着什么?(follow-up to PSA Q&A)

A:
- PSA is not cancer-specific — it reflects prostate epithelial activity/disruption generally.
- HIGH PSA: can signal prostate cancer, but also benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), prostatitis/inflammation, recent ejaculation, catheterization/biopsy/DRE. So a high value is a flag to investigate, not a diagnosis.
- LOW PSA: generally reassuring; also driven down by androgen-deprivation therapy and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride roughly halves PSA — must double the reading).
- Rough clinical anchor: <4 ng/mL traditionally "normal", 4-10 a grey zone, >10 higher cancer suspicion — but thresholds are age/context-dependent and being de-emphasized in favor of kinetics.
- In trials, the KINETICS matter more than a single value:
  - Falling PSA / low nadir after treatment = response (PSA response = >=50% decline).
  - Rising PSA = progression or recurrence (PSA progression per PCWG3).
  - PSA doubling time and time-to-nadir are prognostic.
- Caveat: PSA is a surrogate; PSA changes don't always track OS, and they are easily confounded by concomitant/subsequent anti-cancer therapy.
