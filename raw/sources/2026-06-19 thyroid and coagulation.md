Q: Explain thyroid and coagulation (lab panels).

THYROID
- The thyroid gland makes T4 (thyroxine) and T3 (triiodothyronine) — hormones that set metabolic rate. The pituitary makes TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) to tell the thyroid to produce.
- Feedback loop makes TSH INVERSE to thyroid hormone: when thyroid hormone is low the pituitary pushes harder -> high TSH; when it's high the pituitary backs off -> low TSH.
  - HIGH TSH -> HYPOthyroidism (underactive thyroid).
  - LOW TSH -> HYPERthyroidism (overactive thyroid).
- TSH is the primary screen; free T4 (and free T3) confirm/quantify.
- Why in trials: immunotherapy, TKIs, and radiation / radioligand can cause thyroid dysfunction (thyroiditis, hypo- or hyperthyroidism) -> monitored, sometimes an AESI.

COAGULATION
- The clotting cascade — how blood forms a clot to stop bleeding. The lab tests measure clotting TIME, which reads out bleeding risk.
- PT / INR (prothrombin time / international normalized ratio) — EXTRINSIC + common pathway; sensitive to WARFARIN and to LIVER synthesis (clotting factors are made in the liver, several vitamin-K dependent). INR standardizes PT across labs.
- aPTT / PTT (activated partial thromboplastin time) — INTRINSIC + common pathway; sensitive to HEPARIN and to factor deficiencies (e.g. hemophilia).
- So coagulation doubles as (1) a bleeding-risk assessment and (2) a liver-function readout (because the liver makes the factors). Fibrinogen / D-dimer / platelet count round it out (platelets sit in hematology).
- Why in trials: baseline bleeding risk, anticoagulant monitoring, safety before biopsies/procedures, and signals like DIC.
