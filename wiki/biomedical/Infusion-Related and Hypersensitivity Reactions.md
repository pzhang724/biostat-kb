# Infusion-Related and Hypersensitivity Reactions

A protocol **safety section** covering two overlapping categories of **acute reactions** to an infused/injected product.

- **Infusion-related reaction (IRR, 输注相关反应)** — symptoms **during or shortly after** the IV infusion: fever, chills/rigors (寒战), flushing, hypo-/hypertension, dyspnea (呼吸困难), nausea, back pain. Often **rate-related / cytokine-driven**, *not* a true allergy. Managed by **slowing or pausing** the infusion ± premedication.
- **Hypersensitivity reaction (HSR, 超敏/过敏反应)** — an **immune-mediated allergic** reaction to the drug or excipient: from mild (rash, urticaria 荨麻疹, itching) up to **anaphylaxis** (过敏性休克 — bronchospasm, angioedema, hypotension, shock). Can be IgE-mediated (true allergy) or not.

## Why grouped

At the bedside the two **look alike** and are hard to tell apart in the moment, so protocols handle them together. The section typically specifies:

- recognition + **monitoring** during/after infusion;
- **grading** by [[CTCAE Grade]];
- stepwise **management** — stop/slow infusion; antihistamines, corticosteroids; **epinephrine** + emergency care for anaphylaxis;
- any **premedication** (see [[Anti-Emetic Premedication]] for the related supportive-care idea).

Pre-existing **hypersensitivity** to the drug/excipients is usually an **exclusion criterion** — see [[Eligibility and Safety Parameters (PSMA Radioligand Trial)]]. A safety concept in [[Oncology]].
