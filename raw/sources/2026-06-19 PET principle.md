Human asked: 还有 PET (explain PET, the imaging principle — following the chemotherapy "原理" question).

Answer given (content marker):

PET = Positron Emission Tomography (正电子发射断层成像). It is FUNCTIONAL / MOLECULAR imaging — it maps a biological PROCESS, not just anatomy.

How it works (原理), the chain:
1. Inject a radiotracer (放射性示踪剂) = a biologically active molecule labelled with a positron-emitting isotope (e.g. ¹⁸F-FDG = a glucose analog; ⁶⁸Ga / ¹⁸F PSMA ligands).
2. The isotope decays, emitting a positron (β⁺, 正电子 = the electron's antiparticle).
3. The positron travels a tiny distance, hits an electron → annihilation (湮灭) → their mass converts to TWO 511 keV gamma photons flying out in EXACTLY opposite directions (180°).
4. A ring of detectors around the patient catches the two photons arriving at the same instant = coincidence detection (符合探测) → the event lies on the straight line between those two detectors (line of response).
5. Millions of such lines are reconstructed into a 3-D map of where the tracer concentrated = where the tracked process is happening.

What the signal means depends on the tracer:
- ¹⁸F-FDG: tumours consume more glucose (Warburg effect) → "light up." General oncology PET.
- PSMA ligand: binds PSMA on prostate-cancer cells → see [[PSMA PET-CT]].
Uptake is quantified as SUV (standardized uptake value, 标准摄取值).

Why fused with CT (PET/CT): PET tells you the FUNCTION/where-the-activity-is but is blurry on anatomy; CT supplies the anatomical map. Fused = functional + anatomical in one. (vs CT/MRI alone = anatomy only.)

One line: inject a positron-emitting tracer → positron annihilates with an electron → two back-to-back 511 keV photons → coincidence detection reconstructs a 3-D map of the tracer = a picture of a biological process; tracer choice (FDG vs PSMA) decides what you see.
