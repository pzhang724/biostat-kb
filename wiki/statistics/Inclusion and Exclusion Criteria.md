# Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Together the **eligibility criteria（入选标准）** — they define **who enters the trial**.

- **Inclusion（纳入标准）** = the target population the trial wants to study: the **disease of interest** (right diagnosis / stage / biomarker), demographics（人口学，age/sex）, adequate **organ function** and **performance status**（体能状态，e.g. [[ECOG Performance Status]]）, and ability to **consent and comply**.
- **Exclusion（排除标准）** = among those who meet inclusion, removes a subset — for **three reasons**, and **safety is usually the biggest**, not just "things that affect the result":
  1. **Safety（安全）** — the drug would expose them to undue risk (comorbidities, poor organ function, contraindications, drug interactions). Often the dominant reason.
  2. **Confounding / interpretability（干扰可解释性）** — factors that would muddy the efficacy / safety signal (other active malignancies, concurrent therapies — see [[Prior, Concomitant, and Subsequent Treatment]] — conditions affecting the endpoint).
  3. **Feasibility / data quality（可行性/数据质量）** — can't follow the protocol, pregnancy / lactation, can't undergo the assessments.

**The trade-off**: inclusion + exclusion balance **internal validity（内部有效性 — a clean, homogeneous population → a cleaner signal）** against **generalizability（外推性 — too strict and the result won't apply to real-world patients）**.

So the user's mental model is half right: the **inclusion** half holds; **exclusion** is *not only* about protecting the result — its first purpose is protecting the **patient**, then avoiding confounding, then feasibility.
