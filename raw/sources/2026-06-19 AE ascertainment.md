From chat, 2026-06-19 (follow-up — is AE recording mainly doctor-patient communication?)

Q: Is AE recording mainly relying on doctor-patient communication?

A: For symptomatic AEs, yes — patient communication is the primary channel — but it is not the only one, and it always passes through the investigator's medical judgment.

Two modes of patient communication:
- SPONTANEOUS — the patient volunteers a complaint.
- SOLICITED — at each visit the investigator actively asks, using an open-ended, NON-LEADING question ("Have you had any health problems since your last visit?") rather than reading a symptom checklist — a checklist would bias/inflate reporting. (Structured checklists/diaries are used only for specific solicited safety, e.g. vaccine reactogenicity, or for pre-specified AESIs.)

But AEs also come from non-communication sources:
- Investigator OBSERVATION + physical exam.
- OBJECTIVE tests — an abnormal lab / vital sign / ECG that the investigator judges clinically significant becomes an AE (the data-triggered route).
- Other — ePRO / patient diaries, between-visit phone calls, concomitant-medication review (a new con-med can reveal an unreported AE), hospitalization records.

Common thread: whatever the source, it is the INVESTIGATOR who adjudicates it into an AE record — assigns the term, grade, seriousness, causality, and action taken. So patient communication is the main *input* for symptoms, but the AE record itself is investigator-judged and multi-source.
