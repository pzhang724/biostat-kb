Human's understanding (checking): because tumor is non-measurable, we're forced to look at PSA; if PSA drops, the tumor presumably also shrank; PSA50 = 1 or 0 (binary per patient); this looks like a surrogate endpoint.

Assessment / answer:
- Mostly right, with one important caveat on the middle step.
- "Non-measurable → forced to use PSA": correct as the driver in prostate cancer (bone-predominant / non-measurable disease makes RECIST ORR non-evaluable, so PSA + bone scan are used). Note PSA is often tracked even WHEN there is measurable disease, as a complementary signal — it's not used only as a last resort.
- "If PSA drops, the tumor must have shrunk": this is the shaky link. PSA decline is CORRELATED with tumor burden reduction but not equivalent to it. PSA can fall without radiographic shrinkage, can be lowered disproportionately by some agents, and there's even early "PSA flare" (transient rise despite response). So PSA50 is an INDIRECT readout, not a measurement of tumor size.
- "PSA50 = 1 or 0": yes — per patient it's a binary responder/non-responder flag (confirmed >=50% decline), and PSA-based ORR is the proportion of 1s. The binary-ness is just how the endpoint is summarized.
- "Like a surrogate endpoint": YES, that's exactly the right frame — but be precise about what it's a surrogate FOR. The true clinical endpoint we care about is OS (and to some extent symptoms/QoL). PSA response is a surrogate (biomarker-based) endpoint standing in for that. What makes it a surrogate is that it substitutes for the clinical outcome — NOT the fact that it's binary.
- Crucial regulatory caveat: PSA response is a WEAK / not-fully-validated surrogate for OS in prostate cancer. PSA changes have repeatedly failed to reliably predict survival benefit (trials with PSA improvement but no OS gain, and vice versa). So regulators don't accept PSA response alone; rPFS (radiographic progression-free survival) and OS carry the weight. PCWG3 says report PSA but don't make it the primary efficacy basis.
- Surrogate-validation logic (Prentice criteria spirit): for a marker to be a valid surrogate, the treatment effect on the marker must capture/explain the treatment effect on the true endpoint. PSA hasn't cleanly met that bar in prostate cancer.
