Q: Safety reporting period.

A: The safety reporting period is the protocol-defined window during which AEs/SAEs must be collected and reported. Exact dates are set per protocol, but the standard shape:

- **Start:** usually at **informed consent (签署知情同意)** for SAEs (so SAEs between consent and first dose are captured); AE collection often starts at first dose. Procedures done after consent but before dosing are under observation.
- **End:** a defined period after the **last dose** of study drug — commonly ~28–30 days (varies; longer for long-half-life biologics, radioligands, etc.). This "+30 days" tail catches delayed toxicity.
- **Key exception — related SAEs have no cut-off:** an SAE the investigator assesses as **related (有因果关系)** to the study drug must be reported **whenever** it comes to attention, even long after the standard period ends. Causality, not the calendar, governs.
- **Special always-report items:** some events are reported irrespective of period — e.g. pregnancy, deaths, secondary malignancies — per protocol.

So: AE/SAE collection runs consent/first-dose → last-dose + ~30 days, but a *related* SAE is reportable indefinitely.
