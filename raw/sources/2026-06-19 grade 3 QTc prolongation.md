What it is — the AE term "Electrocardiogram QT corrected interval prolonged" graded by CTCAE.
- QT = time from start of the Q wave to end of the T wave on the [[12-Lead Resting ECG]] = ventricular depolarization + repolarization (one heartbeat's electrical "work").
- QT shortens as heart rate rises, so it's corrected → QTc (rate-corrected; formulas Bazett QTcB, Fridericia QTcF — trials usually prefer QTcF).
- Why it matters: a long QTc means delayed repolarization → risk of torsades de pointes (TdP), a polymorphic VT that can degenerate to VF / sudden death. Many oncology drugs prolong QT, so it's a standard cardiac-safety signal.

CTCAE v5 grading of QTc prolonged:
- Grade 1: QTc 450–480 ms
- Grade 2: QTc 481–500 ms
- Grade 3: QTc ≥501 ms, OR >60 ms change from baseline
- Grade 4: torsade de pointes; polymorphic VT; signs/symptoms of serious arrhythmia

So "Grade 3 QTc" = QTc has reached ≥501 ms (or jumped >60 ms from baseline) but without the serious-arrhythmia event that defines Grade 4. It's the "severe / must act" line (see CTCAE Grade) — triggers dose interruption/reduction, repeat ECGs, electrolyte check (K/Mg/Ca), review of con-meds that prolong QT. ≥501 ms is the widely used red-flag threshold.

Ties together: measured on the 12-lead resting ECG; QTc entry cap (e.g. QTcF ≤480 ms) on the eligibility page is the screen that keeps such patients out; graded via CTCAE; can count toward DLT in dose-finding.
