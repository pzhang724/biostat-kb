# Clinical Progression

**Clinical progression (临床进展)** — the disease getting worse, judged by **clinical signs / symptoms / events**, **not** by measuring the tumour on imaging. Typical triggers:

- New or worse **cancer-related symptoms** — especially pain that needs opioids (阿片类药物) or palliative radiotherapy (姑息性放疗).
- **Symptomatic skeletal events (SSE, 症状性骨骼事件)** — pathologic fracture (病理性骨折), spinal cord compression (脊髓压迫), bone surgery or radiation.
- **Worsening (deterioration, 恶化) of [[ECOG Performance Status|ECOG PS]]**.
- Cancer-related complications (并发症), or a clinical need to start a new anti-cancer therapy because symptoms get worse.

## Relationship to tumour / radiographic progression

Yes, they are related — but **not the same**. Both are "disease progression", measured through different **channels** (途径):

- **Tumour / radiographic progression** (影像学进展) = the tumour clearly (objectively, 客观地) **growing on imaging** (soft tissue by [[RECIST 1.1]], bone by [[PCWG3 Criteria]]) — the basis of **rPFS** (radiographic progression-free survival, 影像学无进展生存期).
- **Clinical progression** = the **patient** getting worse (symptoms, function, clinical events).

[[PCWG3 Criteria]] reports progression **by mode** on purpose (PSA / soft tissue / bone / **symptoms**) as separate endpoints, because the channels can **disagree** (discordant, 不一致):

- a patient can progress clinically (severe pain, worsening ECOG) **before or without** meeting radiographic PD (progressive disease, 疾病进展);
- or show radiographic PD while still having **no symptoms** (asymptomatic, 无症状).

In practice, clinical progression often leads to real-world **stopping of treatment** (discontinuation, 停药) even when imaging has not confirmed progression. In endpoints, **rPFS uses imaging only**, while symptomatic progression / SSE / time to symptomatic progression capture the clinical channel separately. **OS** (overall survival, 总生存期) is the backstop (兜底) that does not depend on any channel.

**Bottom line:** tumour progression is **one channel** (imaging); clinical progression is the **patient-level channel** — the same underlying worsening, seen through a different lens, and the two don't always agree.
