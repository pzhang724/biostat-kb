From chat, 2026-06-19 (follow-up to Best Overall Response)

Q: Why is ORR based on BOR?

A: Because ORR asks a per-patient yes/no question — "did this patient's tumour ever objectively shrink to PR or CR (up to progression)?" — and BOR is exactly the per-patient "best ever achieved" status. So responder = BOR is CR/PR.

Why not a fixed-timepoint response instead:
- Responses occur at DIFFERENT times — some patients shrink by week 6, others by week 24. A single fixed-visit snapshot would miss responders who responded earlier (and may have already progressed) or later (not yet responded at that visit). Taking the best over time captures "ever responded" without depending on when.
- Response is TRANSIENT — tumours shrink then can regrow. A fixed visit would undercount; BOR credits the patient for achieving the response regardless of timing.
- The rules baked into BOR keep it honest: CR/PR must be CONFIRMED (so noise/one-off measurements don't count), SD needs minimum duration, and only up to FIRST PD counts. That makes "BOR = CR/PR" a rigorous, comparable definition of a true responder across patients with different assessment schedules.

Caveat / why ORR isn't the whole story: ORR (built on BOR) tells you WHETHER the tumour responded, not how LONG. Durability is a separate endpoint — DoR (duration of response). So ORR + DoR together; ORR alone is a snapshot of best-achieved.
