# Prohibited and Rescue Medication

Two groups of concomitant medication (合并用药) defined in the protocol — the protocol sets the line between them.

- **Prohibited medication** (禁用药物) — drugs a subject is **not allowed** to take during the trial (or in set time windows), because they would interfere with the study drug, confound (混杂) the endpoint, or raise a safety concern (e.g. other pain drugs in a pain trial; strong CYP, cytochrome P450 细胞色素 P450, inhibitors/inducers 抑制剂/诱导剂). Taking one is a **protocol deviation** (方案偏离) and may lead to stopping treatment or exclusion from a per-protocol analysis (符合方案分析).

- **Rescue medication** (补救药物) — a treatment that is defined in advance and **allowed**, which a subject may take when the study treatment is not controlling symptoms well enough — in acute pain (急性疼痛), an opioid (阿片类) rescue for breakthrough pain (爆发痛). Its use is allowed, but recorded and counted. It is a classic [[Intercurrent Event]] that confounds the efficacy reading, so pain scores after rescue are usually imputed with [[Imputation and Carried-Forward Methods (LOCF, WOCF, wWOCF)]], and rescue use itself becomes an endpoint — the [[Opioid-Free Rate]], total consumption, time to first rescue.

Both are recorded together with [[Prior, Concomitant, and Subsequent Treatment]]. Part of the [[Acute Pain]] thread.
