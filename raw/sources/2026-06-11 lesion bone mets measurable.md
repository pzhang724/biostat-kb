Q: 什么是骨转移,什么是病灶,什么是不可测量?(follow-up in PSA/ORR thread)

A:
- 病灶 (lesion): a localized area of abnormal/diseased tissue — in oncology, a discrete site of tumor. A patient can have many lesions: the primary tumor plus metastases. "Target lesions" are the specific ones chosen to measure and follow over time.
- 骨转移 (bone metastasis): cancer that has spread from the primary organ to bone. Very common in prostate and breast cancer. Shows up on bone scan / CT / MRI as areas of abnormal bone.
- 可测量 vs 不可测量 (measurable vs non-measurable) — a RECIST 1.1 distinction:
  - Measurable: a lesion with a clearly definable diameter you can measure reproducibly — ≥10 mm longest diameter on CT for soft-tissue lesions (≥15 mm short axis for lymph nodes). You can track shrinkage/growth in mm, so it can drive CR/PR/PD assessment.
  - Non-measurable: lesions too small (<10 mm) or with no clean measurable border — and several whole categories that are non-measurable by definition, including BONE METASTASES (lytic/blastic bone disease has no soft-tissue diameter to measure), leptomeningeal disease, ascites, pleural/pericardial effusions, lymphangitic spread.
- Why it matters: RECIST-based ORR needs measurable disease at baseline. Bone-predominant patients (typical in prostate cancer) often have NO measurable target lesion → RECIST ORR not evaluable for them → that's why PSA-based response (PSA50) and bone-scan criteria are used instead.
