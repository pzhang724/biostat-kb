# Lesion, bone mets, measurable disease (chat)

Origin: chat Q&A, 2026-06-11 — `raw/sources/2026-06-11 lesion bone mets measurable.md`. Follow-up in the PSA/ORR thread.

- Question: what are bone metastasis, a lesion, and "non-measurable"?
- Lesion (病灶): a discrete diseased/tumor site; target lesions = the ones chosen to measure over time.
- Bone metastasis (骨转移): cancer spread to bone; common in prostate/breast; seen on bone scan/CT/MRI.
- Measurable (RECIST 1.1): ≥10 mm longest diameter on CT (≥15 mm short axis for nodes) → can drive CR/PR/PD.
- Non-measurable: too small (<10 mm) or no clean border, plus bone mets, leptomeningeal disease, ascites, effusions, lymphangitic spread (non-measurable by definition).
- Link: RECIST ORR needs measurable baseline disease; bone-predominant prostate patients often lack it → PSA50 and bone-scan criteria used instead.

Related follow-up (2026-06-11, `raw/sources/2026-06-11 tumour vs lesion.md`): tumor ≠ lesion exactly. Lesion (病灶) = any localized diseased site (tumor, inflammation, scar…); tumor (肿瘤) = a neoplastic mass. lesion ⊃ tumor. RECIST says "lesion" since some targets are lymph nodes; in cancer trials 病灶 ≈ 肿瘤 colloquially.

Related follow-up (2026-06-11, `raw/sources/2026-06-11 primary vs metastatic lesion.md`): 转移灶 is NOT the original site — it's the new/secondary site cancer spread *to*. 原发灶 (primary) = origin (the prostate); 转移灶 (metastasis) = destination (bone, nodes). 转移 = transfer/move.

Related follow-up (2026-06-11, `raw/sources/2026-06-11 tumour burden multiple lesions.md`): is the tumor one tumor? No — usually primary + multiple metastatic lesions. "Tumor shrank" = total **tumor burden** fell. RECIST tracks the **sum of diameters** of selected target lesions; PSA is a **systemic** marker reflecting aggregate burden (incl. non-measurable bone mets). Either aggregate can hide a **mixed response** (some lesions grow, some shrink).

Related follow-up (2026-06-11, `raw/sources/2026-06-11 why lesion is called bingzao.md`): why 灶? 灶 = hearth/stove; 病灶 = the "hearth" of disease (its focal central point) — same hearth metaphor as Latin *focus* (=fireplace), which "focus of disease" derives from. (English "lesion" is from Latin *laesio* = injury, a different root.)
