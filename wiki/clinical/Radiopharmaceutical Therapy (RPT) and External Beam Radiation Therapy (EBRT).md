# Radiopharmaceutical Therapy (RPT) and External Beam Radiation Therapy (EBRT)

Two ways to deliver **ionizing radiation (电离辐射)** to kill cancer. They differ in **where the radiation source is**, and in how **local or systemic** (局部/全身) the delivery is.

- **EBRT — External Beam Radiation Therapy (外照射放疗)** — radiation aimed at the tumor from a **machine outside the body** (**linear accelerator / linac, 直线加速器**). A **local/regional** treatment of a defined target volume (靶区), given as a **physical procedure** (SDTM **PR** domain, procedures), usually split into many daily sessions (**fractionated, 分次**). Decades of use have given well-known absorbed-dose (吸收剂量) limits for normal organs (**NTCP** models, normal tissue complication probability, 正常组织并发症概率).
- **RPT — Radiopharmaceutical Therapy (放射性药物治疗 / 放射配体治疗)** — a **radioactive drug** given **systemically (全身给药)** (usually IV, sometimes by mouth) that travels in the blood and delivers radiation **from inside the body**. Its ligand (配体) / biology targets it to tumor cells (e.g. [[Lutetium-177 (177Lu)|¹⁷⁷Lu]]-PSMA-617). Dosed by **activity (Bq/GBq, 给药活度)**; absorbed dose in **Gy** (gray, 戈瑞); emits **α / β / γ**. It is a *drug*, recorded as study treatment or concomitant medication (con-med, 合并用药) — not a beam procedure.

**Key contrast:** external source + local target (EBRT) vs internal source + whole-body distribution (RPT). This is exactly why the [[Oncology Therapeutic Radiopharmaceuticals — Dosage Optimization (FDA Draft Guidance)|FDA RPT dosage-optimization guidance]] warns that **organ dose limits from EBRT do not simply transfer to RPTs** — the **dose rate** (剂量率) and the **distribution of radiation** are different.
