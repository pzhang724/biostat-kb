A (clarify + concept):

First, sharpen the example: prostate and lung here are NOT the same expression/target. Prostate
uses PSMA (177Lu-PSMA); lung uses c-Met. They are two different proteins. What is shared is the
STRATEGY, not the molecule: pick patients by a surface/molecular target the tumor overexpresses,
then hit it with a targeted drug. Same playbook, different target.

But the deeper, genuinely striking truth is real: a SINGLE target often IS shared across cancer
types, because cancers are increasingly defined by their molecular driver, not just the organ.
The drug binds a molecule (a protein / mutation / fusion); wherever that molecule is present —
regardless of organ of origin — the drug can act. So one target/agent can span tumors. Examples:
MET appears in lung AND gastric cancer; HER2 in breast/gastric/lung.

This is the basis of basket trials (篮子试验) and tissue-agnostic approvals (组织不可知/泛瘤种获批) —
FDA approvals by biomarker regardless of tumor site: pembrolizumab for MSI-H/dMMR solid tumors;
larotrectinib / entrectinib for NTRK-fusion solid tumors; dabrafenib+trametinib for BRAF V600E
solid tumors. A companion diagnostic (伴随诊断) tests the biomarker to select patients.

One line: organ of origin is becoming less central than the molecular target — same strategy
across prostate and lung, and sometimes literally the same target across organs.
