# Targeted Therapy and Tissue-Agnostic Biomarkers

Why a target-expression biomarker can select patients across very different cancers (prostate, lung…).

## Same strategy ≠ same target

In [[Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer (mCRPC)|prostate]] the selector is [[PSMA PET-CT|PSMA]]; in [[Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)|lung]] it is **c-Met** — **two different proteins**. What is shared is the **strategy (策略)**, not the molecule: pick patients by a **molecular/surface target (分子/表面靶点)** the tumor overexpresses, then hit it with a **targeted drug (靶向药)** (e.g. an antibody-drug conjugate or a radioligand). Same playbook, different target.

## The deeper truth: one target can span organs

Genuinely, a **single target often IS shared across cancer types**, because cancers are increasingly defined by their **molecular driver (驱动基因/分子驱动)**, not just the organ of origin. The drug binds a **molecule** (a protein / mutation / fusion); wherever that molecule is present — regardless of organ — the drug can act. So one target/agent can cover several tumors (e.g. **MET** in lung *and* gastric; **HER2** in breast / gastric / lung).

## Basket trials and tissue-agnostic approvals

This is the basis of:
- **Basket trials (篮子试验)** — one biomarker, many tumor types enrolled together.
- **Tissue-agnostic approvals (组织不可知 / 泛瘤种获批)** — FDA approvals by **biomarker regardless of tumor site**: e.g. **pembrolizumab** for **MSI-H/dMMR** solid tumors; **larotrectinib / entrectinib** for **NTRK-fusion** solid tumors; **dabrafenib + trametinib** for **BRAF V600E** solid tumors.
- A **companion diagnostic (伴随诊断)** tests the biomarker to select patients.

**One line:** organ of origin is becoming less central than the molecular target — the *same strategy* across prostate and lung, and sometimes *literally the same target* across organs.
