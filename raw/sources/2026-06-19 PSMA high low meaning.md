Q: What does high/low PSMA represent?

A: PSMA = Prostate-Specific Membrane Antigen, a protein on the prostate-cell surface, OVEREXPRESSED on prostate cancer cells (more in higher-grade / advanced / mCRPC disease). "High vs low PSMA" = how much of that target the tumour displays, read off PSMA PET-CT as uptake / SUV.

- HIGH PSMA (high uptake / SUV) = the lesion strongly expresses PSMA -> lights up brightly on the scan -> lots of target available.
- LOW / negative PSMA = little/no expression -> doesn't light up.

What it represents / why it matters:
1. Diagnostic — high uptake = PSMA-avid disease; confirms/detects/stages prostate cancer lesions.
2. Therapeutic selection — PSMA is the TARGET for 177Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy (the ligand binds PSMA and delivers radiation). So HIGH PSMA -> more target -> radioligand binds and delivers more dose -> good candidate, more likely to respond (PSMA PET is the eligibility/selection scan; need enough uptake to qualify). LOW/negative PSMA -> little target -> radioligand can't deliver enough dose -> poor candidate / excluded, and such disease can progress without lighting up.
3. Biology — expression tends to be higher in higher-grade, advanced disease, but is HETEROGENEOUS: a patient can have some PSMA+ and some PSMA- lesions.

Caveats: PSMA isn't 100% prostate-specific (physiologic uptake in salivary glands, kidneys, liver, bowel = false positives), and some prostate cancer is PSMA-low (e.g. neuroendocrine / de-differentiated) -> won't respond to PSMA-targeted therapy.

One line: high PSMA = lots of target (avid on PET + good for PSMA radioligand); low PSMA = little target (doesn't light up + poor radioligand candidate).
