Q: RECIST 1.1 和 PCWG3 合在一起怎么决定?(how do the two combine into one decision?)

A:
- In a PCWG3-governed mCRPC trial they don't compete — each governs a DIFFERENT disease compartment, and the trial combines them into a composite radiographic endpoint, rPFS (radiographic progression-free survival).
- Two radiographic channels:
  - Soft tissue / lymph nodes -> assessed by RECIST 1.1 (PD = >=20% SoD rise from nadir +>=5 mm, or new soft-tissue lesion).
  - Bone -> assessed by PCWG3 bone-scan "2+2 rule" (>=2 new lesions, confirmed by >=2 more on the next scan).
- Combination logic = OR / earliest-event: radiographic progression is declared when EITHER soft-tissue PD (RECIST) OR bone PD (2+2) occurs — whichever happens first. Plus death counts as an rPFS event.
- Date assignment nuance for bone: when the 2+2 rule is met, the progression DATE is back-dated to the FIRST scan that showed the (first) 2 new lesions, not the confirmation scan.
- PSA and symptoms are NOT folded into the radiographic decision. PSA progression (>=25% & >=2 ng/mL above nadir, confirmed) and symptomatic endpoints are tracked/reported SEPARATELY. You do not declare progression on rising PSA alone in PCWG3.
- So the "overall decision" hierarchy in practice: any one compartment progressing (soft tissue OR bone) -> radiographic progression -> rPFS event; PSA-only or symptom changes are separate readouts, and OS is the ultimate endpoint.
- Why split this way: bone, soft tissue, PSA, and symptoms can move independently (mixed response, flare), so PCWG3 keeps them as distinct endpoints rather than one lumped PD, while still giving one composite (rPFS) for the primary analysis.
