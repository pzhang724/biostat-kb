# MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)

**MRI (磁共振成像)** — anatomical imaging with the best **soft-tissue** contrast, and crucially **no ionizing radiation** (uses magnetism + radio waves, unlike X-ray / CT / [[PET (Positron Emission Tomography)|PET]]).

## How it works (原理)

1. The body is full of **hydrogen protons (氢质子)** — mostly in water and fat; each spins like a tiny magnet.
2. A **strong magnetic field (强磁场,** e.g. 1.5T / 3T) aligns their spins.
3. A **radiofrequency (RF, 射频)** pulse tips the spins out of alignment.
4. When the pulse stops, protons **relax** back and re-emit RF signal. The relaxation rate differs by tissue — constants **T1 and T2** — so tissues differ in signal = **contrast**.
5. **Gradient fields (梯度磁场)** encode position → reconstruct a spatial image.

**Why it's used:**
- Superb soft-tissue contrast — brain, prostate, liver, **bone marrow**, spinal cord; better than CT for these.
- No radiation → safe to repeat / serial follow-up.
- Functional variants: **DWI (弥散加权** — cellularity; picks up tumour & marrow mets), DCE (perfusion). Contrast agent = **gadolinium (钆)**, not iodine.
- Prostate cancer: multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) for local staging; whole-body MRI/DWI for bone-marrow metastases.

**Trade-offs:** slower and noisier than CT, motion-sensitive, contraindicated with some metal implants / pacemakers.

## Modality contrast

- **CT** — X-ray attenuation; fast, great for bone/lung/anatomy; uses radiation.
- **MRI** — magnetism + RF; best soft tissue; no radiation; slower.
- **PET** — positron-emitting tracer; **functional/molecular** (what tissue is *doing*); usually fused as PET/CT.

So **CT/MRI = anatomy** (MRI better soft tissue, no radiation), **PET = function/biology**. An imaging modality used in [[Radiological Assessment]] within [[Oncology]].
