# Anti-Tumour Activity

Evidence that a treatment **works against the cancer** (抗肿瘤活性) — it shrinks tumours, slows or stops their growth, or in some other way reduces tumour burden (肿瘤负荷). It is the **efficacy signal** (疗效信号), and it is the wording used especially in **early-phase (Phase I/II)** trials for early / exploratory efficacy, before confirmatory endpoints (确证性终点).

**How it's shown:** objective response (客观缓解 — tumour shrinkage by [[RECIST 1.1]] → ORR, objective response rate; [[Best Overall Response]]), depth / duration of response (DoR, 缓解持续时间), disease control (疾病控制, DCR), a fall in [[PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)|PSA]] in prostate cancer, [[Progression-Free Survival (PFS) and Overall Survival (OS)|PFS]] (无进展生存).

**Activity vs clinical benefit (活性 vs 临床获益)** — a key distinction. *Activity* is a weaker claim (the drug does something to the tumour); *clinical benefit* (e.g. OS 总生存, symptoms / quality of life) means it really helps the patient live longer or better. A drug can show anti-tumour activity without yet proving clinical benefit — see [[Surrogate Endpoint]].

**Examples of what might count as anti-tumour activity:**

1. The sum of target-lesion diameters shrinks ≥30% → a partial response (RECIST PR, 部分缓解); lesions disappear completely = CR (complete response, 完全缓解).
2. 40% of patients in a cohort reach CR/PR → ORR 40%.
3. Prostate cancer: PSA falls ≥50% from baseline (PSA50).
4. Imaging shows less tumour burden — no new lesions on bone scan, lower tracer uptake on [[PSMA PET-CT]].
5. Responses last longer / progression comes later than expected (a PFS gain); a waterfall plot (瀑布图) showing that most patients have some tumour shrinkage.

The common thread: all are direct signs that the **tumour itself is pushed back** (shrinkage, a falling marker, control) — not yet proof that the patient lives longer (clinical benefit / OS).

An efficacy concept used across [[Oncology]].
