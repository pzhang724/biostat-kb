Human asked: 为什么 ECG 是 12-lead ECG?

Answer given (content marker):

"12-lead" = 12 different electrical views/angles of the same heart, not 12 separate machines. From 10 electrodes the machine computes 12 leads:
- 6 limb leads (frontal plane): I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF
- 6 precordial/chest leads (horizontal plane): V1–V6

Why so many angles:
- One lead = one direction only; it can miss a problem that points another way. Multiple angles let you SEE the whole heart and LOCALIZE an abnormality to a region — anterior / inferior / lateral / septal wall (e.g. which artery's territory is ischemic/infarcted), which side/chamber is enlarged, where a conduction block sits.
- 12-lead is the standard *complete diagnostic* ECG (vs a 1–3 lead rhythm strip / telemetry, which only tracks rate & rhythm).

Why trials specify it:
- Cardiac-safety regulation expects the full 12-lead. QT/QTc measurement and the ICH E14 (thorough-QT / QT assessment) framework are built on standardized 12-lead recordings — complete, reproducible, comparable across sites and visits. A rhythm strip wouldn't give reliable interval/morphology data.
- "Resting" + full 12-lead = the standardized condition so QTc and morphology are comparable visit-to-visit.

Short version: 12 leads = 12 viewing angles → you can both detect AND localize, and it's the regulatory standard for the QT/QTc cardiac-safety read. Ties to the QTc eligibility cap.
