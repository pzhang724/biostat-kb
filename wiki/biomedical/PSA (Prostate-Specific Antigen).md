# PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)

**PSA (prostate-specific antigen / 前列腺特异性抗原)** is a glycoprotein secreted by prostate epithelial cells. It is secreted into semen at high concentration; only a small amount leaks into the bloodstream, and clinically "PSA" means the **serum PSA** level (ng/mL) measured from a blood sample. A rising serum value reflects more PSA leaking out as prostate tissue architecture is disrupted (cancer, inflammation, BPH). Total, free, and complexed forms are reported; the free/total ratio helps separate cancer from BPH in the grey zone. (Distinct from **PSMA**, a cell-surface target used for imaging — see [[PSMA PET-CT]].) It is the most widely used **biomarker** in prostate cancer — for screening and monitoring, and in clinical trials as an efficacy and progression endpoint.

As a trial endpoint:

- **PSA response** — a decline of ≥50% from baseline.
- **PSA progression** — defined per the **PCWG3** (Prostate Cancer Working Group 3) criteria.
- Typically reported alongside imaging-based endpoints (RECIST, bone scan / PET).

## What high vs low PSA means

PSA is **not cancer-specific** — it reflects prostate epithelial activity/disruption in general, so a level is a flag, not a diagnosis.

- **High PSA** — can signal prostate cancer, but also benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), prostatitis/inflammation, or recent ejaculation/instrumentation (biopsy, catheter, DRE). Prompts investigation, not a verdict.
- **Low PSA** — generally reassuring; also driven down by androgen-deprivation therapy and 5-α-reductase inhibitors (finasteride roughly halves PSA, so the reading must be doubled).
- **Rough anchor** — <4 ng/mL traditionally "normal", 4–10 a grey zone, >10 higher suspicion — but thresholds are age/context-dependent and increasingly de-emphasized in favor of kinetics.

In trials the **kinetics** matter more than any single value: a falling PSA / low post-treatment nadir indicates response (PSA response = ≥50% decline), a rising PSA indicates progression or recurrence (per [[PCWG3 Criteria]]), and PSA doubling time / time-to-nadir are prognostic.

**ORR based on PSA** — an objective response rate defined by a PSA criterion instead of RECIST imaging: the proportion of patients with a confirmed PSA decline of ≥50% from baseline (**PSA50**; PSA30/PSA90 also reported). Useful in prostate cancer because many patients (especially mCRPC) have bone-predominant or [[Measurable vs Non-Measurable Disease (RECIST)|non-measurable disease]] where RECIST-based ORR isn't evaluable. It remains a binary proportion endpoint (responders / evaluable-baseline-PSA), analyzed like any ORR (point estimate + exact CI; derived per patient via [[Best Overall Response]]). PCWG3 caveat: PSA response is a surrogate, not a substitute for radiographic response or OS — report it (e.g. waterfall of max PSA decline) but don't rely on it alone as the primary efficacy measure. Caveat: PSA is a **[[Surrogate Endpoint|surrogate]]** — its changes don't always track OS and are easily confounded by other anti-cancer therapy.

PSA is exactly the kind of efficacy measure that is easily contaminated by concomitant or subsequent treatment: changes must be read in light of what other anti-cancer therapy the patient received (see [[Anti-Cancer Therapy Categories in Oncology Trials]]).
