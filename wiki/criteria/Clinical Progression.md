# Clinical Progression

**Clinical progression (临床进展)** — the disease getting worse judged by **clinical signs / symptoms / events**, **not** by imaging measurement of the tumour. Typical triggers:

- New or worsening **cancer-related symptoms** — esp. pain needing opioids or palliative radiotherapy.
- **Symptomatic skeletal events (SSE)** — pathologic fracture, spinal cord compression, bone surgery/radiation.
- **Deterioration in [[ECOG Performance Status|ECOG PS]]**.
- Cancer-related complications, or a clinical need to start new anti-cancer therapy for symptomatic decline.

## Relationship to tumour / radiographic progression

Yes, related — but **not the same**. Both are "disease progression," measured through different **channels**:

- **Tumour / radiographic progression** = the tumour objectively **growing on imaging** (soft tissue by [[RECIST 1.1]], bone by [[PCWG3 Criteria]]) — the basis of **rPFS**.
- **Clinical progression** = the **patient** worsening (symptoms, function, clinical events).

[[PCWG3 Criteria]] deliberately reports progression **by mode** (PSA / soft tissue / bone / **symptoms**) as separate endpoints, because the channels can be **discordant**:

- a patient can clinically progress (severe pain, falling ECOG) **before/without** meeting radiographic PD;
- or show radiographic PD while still **asymptomatic**.

In practice clinical progression often drives real-world **discontinuation** even when imaging hasn't confirmed. In endpoints, **rPFS is radiographic-only**, while symptomatic progression / SSE / time-to-symptomatic-progression capture the clinical channel separately; **OS** is the channel-independent backstop.

**Bottom line:** tumour progression is **one channel** (imaging); clinical progression is the **patient-level channel** — same underlying worsening, different lens, and they don't always agree. See [[Radiological Assessment]] and [[Best Overall Response]]; a progression concept in [[Oncology]].
