Answer captured:

- LAST (Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity): a serious adverse reaction when a local anesthetic
  (bupivacaine, lidocaine, ropivacaine) reaches toxic systemic concentrations — from accidental
  intravascular injection or excess dose/absorption. Two-system toxicity: CNS (early perioral
  numbness, tinnitus, metallic taste, agitation -> seizures -> CNS depression/coma) and
  cardiovascular (hypotension, arrhythmias, conduction block, cardiac arrest; bupivacaine is
  especially cardiotoxic). Relevant in acute pain trials using local anesthetics for post-surgical
  analgesia (long-acting bupivacaine wound infiltration / nerve block). Management: stop injection,
  airway/seizure control, and IV lipid emulsion ("lipid rescue" / Intralipid). A key safety event /
  AESI to monitor.

- Wound healing: post-surgical recovery of the incision; in acute pain trials it's a safety
  outcome — the analgesic (especially one infiltrated/instilled at the surgical site, or NSAIDs)
  must not impair healing. Assessed by wound-healing status / complications (dehiscence, infection,
  delayed healing). Particularly scrutinized for locally administered drugs at the wound.
