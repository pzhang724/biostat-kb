Human asked: 说一下 chemotherapy 的原理，为什么是 therapy。

Answer given (content marker):

Principle (原理): chemotherapy = cytotoxic (细胞毒) drugs that kill cells by disrupting the machinery of cell DIVISION. Cancer's defining trait is uncontrolled rapid proliferation, so cells that divide fast are more vulnerable — chemo exploits that difference. Main classes by what step they hit:
- Alkylating agents (烷化剂) — cross-link / damage DNA so it can't replicate (e.g. cisplatin-like, cyclophosphamide).
- Antimetabolites (抗代谢药) — fake building blocks that block DNA/RNA synthesis (e.g. methotrexate, 5-FU, gemcitabine).
- Anti-microtubule / spindle poisons (微管抑制剂) — block the mitotic spindle so the cell can't split (taxanes e.g. docetaxel; vinca alkaloids).
- Topoisomerase inhibitors (拓扑异构酶抑制剂) — jam DNA unwinding enzymes.
- Anti-tumour antibiotics (抗肿瘤抗生素) — e.g. anthracyclines, DNA intercalation + free radicals.
Net effect: damage DNA / stop mitosis → trigger apoptosis. It is SYSTEMIC (travels everywhere via blood), unlike local surgery/radiotherapy.

Why the toxicity: chemo is NOT cancer-specific — it hits any fast-dividing cell, so the classic side effects are in normally fast-renewing tissues: bone marrow (cytopenias), GI lining (mucositis, nausea), hair follicles (alopecia). That non-selectivity is the core limitation, and the reason newer "targeted"/immune therapies were developed.

Why is it called "therapy" (为什么叫 therapy):
- "-therapy" (疗法 / 治疗) simply means a treatment modality given to achieve clinical benefit — same suffix as radiotherapy, immunotherapy, hormone therapy. It denotes "a way of treating disease," not a drug per se.
- "Chemo-therapy" literally = "treatment by chemical (agents)." The term comes from Paul Ehrlich (early 1900s), who coined it for using chemicals to kill the disease-causing agent selectively.
- So it is "therapy" because it is one of the MODALITIES of cancer treatment — the systemic-drug modality — sitting alongside the other modalities: surgery (手术), radiotherapy (放疗), and the newer systemic ones (targeted therapy, immunotherapy, hormone/endocrine therapy, and radioligand therapy). Calling it "therapy" places it as a treatment approach in that lineup, defined by mechanism (chemical/cytotoxic), not by drug name.
