# IRB and IEC (Ethics Committees)

**IRB** (Institutional Review Board, 机构审查委员会) and **IEC** (Independent Ethics Committee, 独立伦理委员会) are **the same function under two names** — the independent committee that protects the **rights, safety and well-being** of human trial subjects. **IRB** = the US term (FDA **21 CFR 56**; CFR = US Code of Federal Regulations); **IEC** = the **ICH / EU** term ([[Investigator's Brochure (IB)|ICH E6 GCP]]).

## What they do

- **Before the start** — review and **approve** the protocol, the **informed consent form (ICF, 知情同意书)**, the [[Investigator's Brochure (IB)]], recruitment materials, and the investigators' qualifications; weigh **risk-benefit** (风险-获益); make sure informed consent is adequate.
- **Ongoing** — **continuing review** (持续审查; at least once a year), review of [[Protocol Amendment|amendments]], receiving **safety reports** ([[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#SUSAR — the reportable subset|SUSARs]] / expedited) and progress reports; they can **suspend or end** (terminate) the approval.
- **Composition** (组成) — independent members, including **scientific and non-scientific / lay** (非专业) community members (details below).

## Who's on it

Per ICH E6 GCP / FDA **21 CFR 56.107**:

- **At least 5 members**, with **mixed backgrounds** — scientific **and** non-scientific.
- **≥1 non-scientific** member (e.g. ethicist 伦理学家, lawyer 律师, clergy 神职人员).
- **≥1 member who is independent** of the institution or site (not affiliated with it) — a **community / lay** (社区/非专业) representative.
- Diversity (gender, cultural/community background); for **vulnerable populations** (弱势人群), a member with the relevant expertise.
- Typical roles: clinicians, a scientist/methodologist (方法学家; sometimes a **statistician**), a **pharmacist** (药剂师), an ethicist, a legal expert, a lay/community member, often a **patient advocate** (患者代表).
- **Conflict of interest** (利益冲突): a member with a conflict on a given study **steps out of** (recuses from, 回避) its vote. Two groups: **financial** (money/equity/proprietary interest — also the [[Financial Disclosure (Clinical Investigators)|21 CFR 54]] axis) and **non-financial / competing professional interest** (e.g. the member **runs another trial of the same type / a rival program** — they compete for the same patients, plus there is a **confidentiality** (保密) risk from seeing a competitor's protocol; also personal relationships, an intellectual stake). A competing trial of the same type falls into the **non-financial** group → **disclose + recuse**. (It becomes financial too only if they also hold equity / outcome-linked payment in the rival product.)

## The questions they answer

One big question — **"is it ethical to enroll humans in this study?"** — mapped onto the three **Belmont** principles (贝尔蒙特原则):

- **Respect for persons (尊重个人) → informed consent** — is the consent process / ICF adequate, complete and understandable (risks, alternatives, voluntary, right to withdraw), and properly documented? Is recruitment **free of pressure** (non-coercive, 非强迫) and is any payment reasonable (no **undue inducement**, 不当诱导)?
- **Beneficence (有利/善行) → risk-benefit + safety** — are the risks **minimized** and **reasonable** compared with the expected benefit and the knowledge gained? Is there adequate **safety monitoring** (DSMB, data safety monitoring board 数据安全监察委员会; stopping rules)? Is the science **sound enough to justify** exposing humans (a worthless study cannot be ethical)?
- **Justice (公正) → fair selection** — is the selection of subjects **fair** (equitable)? Are there extra **safeguards for vulnerable populations** (children, pregnant women, people with cognitive impairment 认知障碍, prisoners, the economically disadvantaged)?

Plus: are **privacy / confidentiality** protected? Are the investigators and the site qualified? Any **conflicts of interest**? (A separate COI lens, aimed at the regulator, is [[Financial Disclosure (Clinical Investigators)]].)

**Boundary:** they focus on **protecting subjects**, not on optimizing the science/statistics (that is the job of the sponsor/regulator). Scientific validity matters only because a study too flawed to give useful knowledge cannot justify the risk — they don't redesign the SAP.

## Why their authority is binding

- **An ethical foundation built on past abuses (滥用)** — Nuremberg Code (纽伦堡守则) → Declaration of Helsinki (赫尔辛基宣言) → Belmont Report (respect for persons, beneficence, justice). The lesson: research on humans needs an **independent review beforehand**.
- **It counters a built-in conflict of interest** — investigators and sponsors have scientific ambition and funding at stake, so an **independent** body (not the people running the trial) must protect the subjects.
- **Required by law, not just advice** — no FDA-regulated trial may go ahead without IRB approval (21 CFR 56); ICH GCP and the Declaration of Helsinki require IEC approval. Their decisions — **approve, require changes, disapprove, suspend/end** — are **binding** (有约束力).
- **Independence + binding power is the point** — being able to say **no** is what makes subject protection enforceable (可执行). They are the **gate** between the protocol and any human being enrolled.

## Two separate gates

A site **cannot enroll** without ethics approval. **Ethics approval (IRB/IEC)** and **regulatory authorization** (监管授权; from the authority, e.g. FDA **IND**, investigational new drug application) are **two separate gates — both are required**. The ethics committee is also one of the receivers in the SUSAR [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#Reporting flow to the regulatory authority|reporting flow]].

## CIOMS (related)

**CIOMS** (Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences, 国际医学科学组织理事会) is an international NGO (non-governmental organization, 非政府组织; under WHO/UNESCO) — **not** an oversight committee. In drug safety it means the **CIOMS Form** (CIOMS I), the de-facto international standard **paper form for an expedited individual case safety report (ICSR, 个例安全性报告)** — the human-readable counterpart of an **ICH E2B** electronic report, used to send SUSARs/SAEs to authorities and ethics committees. CIOMS working groups also wrote foundational **pharmacovigilance** (药物警戒) guidance (CIOMS I–VIII) and international **ethics** guidelines for health-related research. So CIOMS is about **how a SUSAR/SAE is sent**, which is different from the IRB/IEC ethics gate. A trial-conduct concept used across [[Oncology]].
