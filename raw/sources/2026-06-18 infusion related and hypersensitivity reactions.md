Excerpt: "Potential Infusion Related and Hypersensitivity Reactions"

A (summary delivered in chat):

A protocol safety section covering two overlapping categories of ACUTE reactions to an
infused/injected product:

- Infusion-related reaction (IRR, 输注相关反应): symptoms during or shortly after the IV infusion
  — fever, chills/rigors (寒战), flushing, hypo-/hypertension, dyspnea, nausea, back pain. Often
  rate-related / cytokine-driven, not a true allergy. Managed by slowing or pausing the infusion
  ± premedication.
- Hypersensitivity reaction (HSR, 超敏/过敏反应): an immune-mediated allergic reaction to the drug
  or excipient — from mild (rash, urticaria 荨麻疹, itching) up to anaphylaxis (bronchospasm,
  angioedema, hypotension, shock). Can be IgE-mediated (true allergy) or not.

Why grouped: at the bedside the two look alike and are hard to tell apart in the moment, so
protocols handle them together. The section typically specifies: recognition, monitoring during/
after infusion, CTCAE grading, and stepwise management (stop/slow infusion; antihistamines,
corticosteroids; epinephrine + emergency care for anaphylaxis), plus any premedication. Pre-
existing hypersensitivity to the drug/excipients is usually an exclusion criterion.
