# Anti-Emetic Premedication

An **anti-emetic (止吐) premedication** regimen — drugs given **prophylactically** (预防性, before the fact) to prevent **nausea and vomiting** (恶心呕吐) around an infusion. A typical protocol line: *"dexamethasone and domperidone/metoclopramide or institutional anti-emetic regimen."*

- **Dexamethasone (地塞米松)** — a corticosteroid (糖皮质激素) used as an anti-emetic (and anti-inflammatory) premedication.
- **Domperidone (多潘立酮) / Metoclopramide (甲氧氯普胺)** — prokinetic **dopamine D2-receptor antagonists** (多巴胺受体拮抗剂), classic anti-emetics. The "/" = either one (often by site availability; domperidone has less CNS penetration, metoclopramide is more widely available).
- **"or institutional anti-emetic regimen"** — the site may instead use its own hospital standard anti-emetic protocol: flexibility, while still ensuring prophylaxis happens.

## Why here

In [[Lutetium-177 (177Lu)|177Lu]]-PSMA radioligand therapy the dose is **co-infused with amino acids** (arginine/lysine) for **renal protection** (肾保护), which very commonly cause nausea/vomiting — so anti-emetic premedication is standard supportive care. Prophylaxis, not after-the-fact treatment.
