Q: 讲一下 prognostic covariate adjustment 这一类的概念，prognostic score

A: Prognostic covariate adjustment refers to using a baseline covariate (or a composite "prognostic score" predicting outcome under control, independent of treatment) in the primary analysis model (typically ANCOVA) to increase precision of the treatment-effect estimate without introducing bias, since randomization already balances treatment assignment. The prognostic score compresses several baseline covariates into a single number — often generated by a model trained on historical/external control-arm data — so you can adjust for it without spending many degrees of freedom on individual covariates. PROCOVA (PROgnostic COVariate Adjustment) is the best-known implementation: a pre-specified, pre-trained ML model computes each participant's predicted outcome from baseline data, and that one score enters the ANCOVA as the sole covariate, "locked" before unblinding to avoid data-dredging. Regulators (FDA's 2023 covariate-adjustment guidance) generally support the principle: adjusting for prognostic baseline covariates raises power / reduces required sample size without unblinding-related bias, as long as covariates are pre-specified. The unresolved point in the field is validating the external prognostic model against the trial's own control arm before relying on it.
