Q: Difference between prostate cancer history and medical history.

A: Same kind of data (past/concurrent conditions), but split by whether it's the DISEASE UNDER STUDY or everything else.

- DISEASE HISTORY / Disease Characteristics (= "prostate cancer history") = the history of the INDEX disease the trial is treating: date of initial diagnosis, histology, Gleason/grade, initial & current TNM/stage, metastatic sites, prior prostate-cancer treatments, PSA/castration status. Drives eligibility, staging, line of therapy, stratification, and the efficacy baseline.
- MEDICAL HISTORY (MH) = the patient's GENERAL past and concurrent medical conditions / comorbidities NOT the study disease: hypertension, diabetes, prior MI, other surgeries, allergies. Background health, mainly for safety context and contraindications.

The dividing line: "is it the cancer we're treating, or background health?" Disease-under-study -> disease history; everything else -> medical history.

SDTM mapping: both commonly live in the MH domain, distinguished by MHCAT / MHSCAT (e.g. "PROSTATE CANCER" / oncology history vs "GENERAL MEDICAL HISTORY"); the disease characteristics are often collected on a separate disease-history CRF even if they map back to MH.
