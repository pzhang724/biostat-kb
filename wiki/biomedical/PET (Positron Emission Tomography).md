# PET (Positron Emission Tomography)

**PET (正电子发射断层成像)** — **functional / molecular** imaging: it maps a biological **process**, not just anatomy.

## How it works (原理)

1. Inject a **radiotracer (放射性示踪剂)** = a biologically active molecule labelled with a **positron-emitting** isotope (¹⁸F-FDG = a glucose analog; ⁶⁸Ga / ¹⁸F PSMA ligands).
2. The isotope decays, emitting a **positron (β⁺, 正电子** = the electron's antiparticle).
3. The positron travels a tiny distance, hits an electron → **annihilation (湮灭)** → their mass becomes **two 511 keV gamma photons** flying out in exactly **opposite directions (180°)**.
4. A ring of detectors catches the two photons at the same instant = **coincidence detection (符合探测)** → the event lies on the line between those detectors (line of response).
5. Millions of such lines reconstruct a 3-D map of where the tracer concentrated = where the tracked process is happening. Uptake quantified as **SUV (standardized uptake value, 标准摄取值)**.

**What the signal means = the tracer:**
- **¹⁸F-FDG** — tumours consume more glucose (Warburg effect) → "light up." General oncology PET.
- **PSMA ligand** — binds PSMA on prostate-cancer cells → see [[PSMA PET-CT]].

**Why fused with CT (PET/CT):** PET shows function but is anatomically blurry; CT supplies the anatomical map → functional + anatomical in one. Contrast with [[MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)]] / CT, which show anatomy only.

Theranostic link: the same PSMA target images *and* treats ([[Lutetium-177 (177Lu)]]-PSMA; radiation quantified by [[Dosimetry]]). An imaging modality used in [[Radiological Assessment]] within [[Oncology]].
