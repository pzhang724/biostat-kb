Human asked: Clinical progression 是什么意思,跟 tumor progression 有关系吗?

Answer given (content marker):

Clinical progression (临床进展) = the disease getting worse judged by CLINICAL signs / symptoms / events — NOT by imaging measurement of the tumour. Typical triggers:
- New or worsening cancer-related symptoms — esp. pain needing opioids or palliative radiotherapy.
- Skeletal-related / symptomatic skeletal events (SSE): pathologic fracture, spinal cord compression, need for bone surgery/radiation.
- Deterioration in performance status (ECOG worsening).
- Cancer-related complications (e.g. obstruction), or clinical need to start a new anti-cancer therapy for symptomatic decline.

Tumour / radiographic progression = the tumour objectively GROWING on imaging — soft tissue by RECIST, bone by PCWG3 — i.e. measured disease, the basis of rPFS.

Relationship — yes, related but NOT the same. Both are forms of "disease progression," just measured through different CHANNELS:
- tumour/radiographic = imaging of the tumour;
- clinical = the patient (symptoms, function, clinical events).
PCWG3 deliberately reports progression BY MODE (PSA / soft tissue / bone / symptoms) as separate endpoints, precisely because these channels can be DISCORDANT:
- a patient can clinically progress (severe pain, falling ECOG) before/without meeting radiographic PD;
- or show radiographic PD while still feeling fine (asymptomatic).
In practice clinical progression often drives real-world treatment discontinuation even when imaging hasn't confirmed; in endpoints, rPFS is radiographic-only, while symptomatic progression / SSE / time-to-symptomatic-progression capture the clinical channel separately. OS is the channel-independent backstop.

Bottom line: tumour progression is ONE channel (imaging); clinical progression is the patient-level channel. Same underlying disease worsening, different lens, and they don't always agree.
