From chat, 2026-06-19 (follow-up to IRB/IEC)

Q: What questions do IRB/IEC typically answer?

A: One overarching question — "is it ethical to enroll humans in this study?" — broken down, and mapping onto the three Belmont principles:

Respect for persons (informed consent):
- Is the informed consent process and the ICF adequate, complete, and understandable (risks, alternatives, voluntary, right to withdraw)? Is consent properly documented?
- Is recruitment non-coercive, and is any payment reasonable (no undue inducement)?

Beneficence (risk-benefit + safety):
- Are risks MINIMIZED and REASONABLE relative to anticipated benefits and the knowledge to be gained?
- Is there adequate safety monitoring (DSMB, stopping rules)?
- Is the science sound enough to justify exposing humans? (A worthless study can't be ethical — no value to offset the risk.)

Justice (fair selection):
- Is subject selection EQUITABLE — inclusion/exclusion fair, not over-burdening some groups?
- Are there extra SAFEGUARDS for vulnerable populations (children, pregnant, cognitively impaired, prisoners, economically disadvantaged)?

Plus: are privacy/confidentiality protected? Are the investigators and site qualified? Any conflicts of interest?

Important boundary: they focus on SUBJECT PROTECTION, not on optimizing the science/statistics (that's the sponsor/regulator). Scientific validity matters only insofar as a study too flawed to yield useful knowledge can't justify the risk — they don't redesign the SAP.
