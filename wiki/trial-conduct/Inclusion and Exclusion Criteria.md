# Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Together they are the **eligibility criteria (入选标准)** — they define **who enters the trial**.

- **Inclusion (纳入标准)** = the target population that the trial wants to study: the **disease of interest** (the right diagnosis / stage / biomarker), demographics (人口学: age/sex), good enough **organ function** and **performance status** (体能状态, e.g. [[ECOG Performance Status]]), and the ability to **consent and comply** (知情同意并依从).
- **Exclusion (排除标准)** = among the people who meet inclusion, it removes a subset — for **three reasons**, and **safety is usually the biggest one**, not just "things that affect the result":
  1. **Safety (安全)** — the drug would put them at too much risk (comorbidities 合并症, poor organ function, contraindications 禁忌证, drug interactions). Often the main reason.
  2. **Confounding / interpretability (干扰可解释性)** — factors that would muddy the efficacy / safety signal (other active cancers, concurrent therapies — see [[Prior, Concomitant, and Subsequent Treatment]] — conditions that affect the endpoint).
  3. **Feasibility / data quality (可行性/数据质量)** — cannot follow the protocol, pregnancy / breast-feeding (lactation, 哺乳期), cannot undergo the assessments.

**The trade-off (权衡)**: inclusion + exclusion balance **internal validity (内部有效性 — a clean, similar population → a cleaner signal)** against **generalizability (外推性 — if the criteria are too strict, the result won't apply to real-world patients)**.

So the human's mental model was half right: the **inclusion** half holds; **exclusion** is *not only* about protecting the result — its first purpose is protecting the **patient**, then avoiding confounding, then feasibility.
