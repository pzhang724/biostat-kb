# Safety vs Tolerability

Two distinct trial objectives, usually reported together ("safety and tolerability") as the typical primary objective of phase I.

- **Safety (安全性)** — what harm the drug does, the objective medical risk. Measured by adverse events (AEs), serious AEs ([[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)]]), lab abnormalities, vital signs, ECG/QTc ([[12-Lead Resting ECG]]), deaths; characterized by the **nature, frequency, severity** ([[CTCAE Grade]]), and **causality** of those events. Question it answers: *does the drug injure the body, and how badly?*
- **Tolerability (耐受性)** — how well the patient can put up with the drug and **stay on treatment** as planned, i.e. whether the side effects are manageable enough to keep dosing. Measured by **dose reductions, dose interruptions/delays, and discontinuations due to AEs** (the [[Action Taken for an Adverse Event]] fields), plus treatment compliance/adherence. Question it answers: *can the patient stand to keep taking it on schedule?*

## Relationship

Safety = the objective harm profile; tolerability = the practical/subjective ability to live with it and stay on the regimen. They come apart: a drug can be **safe** (no serious organ damage) but **poorly tolerated** (constant nausea/fatigue → people stop), or fairly **well tolerated** yet carry rare **serious safety** risks.
