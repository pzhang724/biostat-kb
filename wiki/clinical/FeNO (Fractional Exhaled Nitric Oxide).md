# FeNO (Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide)

**FeNO** = fractional exhaled nitric oxide (呼出气一氧化氮分数) — a **non-invasive (无创) biomarker** measured in the breath you breathe out, reported in **ppb** (parts per billion, 十亿分率).

- It reflects **type 2 / eosinophilic (嗜酸性) airway inflammation**: IL-13 (interleukin-13, 白细胞介素-13) turns up (upregulates, 上调) iNOS (inducible nitric oxide synthase, 诱导型一氧化氮合酶) in the airway lining (epithelium, 上皮), which makes more NO.
- **High FeNO** signals eosinophilic inflammation and usually predicts a good response to **inhaled corticosteroids (ICS)**.
- Used to **phenotype** (分型) asthma, to **select patients** for type 2 biologics (e.g. anti-IL-4Rα / IL-13), and to **monitor** inflammation and whether patients take their treatment (adherence, 依从性).

**Direction**: lower FeNO is better. High FeNO means heavier type 2 / eosinophilic inflammation, and effective anti-inflammatory treatment (ICS, anti-IL-4Rα / IL-13 biologics) brings it down.

**Change from baseline of FeNO** is different from the [[Pre-BD and Post-BD FEV1]] endpoint:

- **Opposite direction** — a *fall* in FeNO is the good outcome.
- **Shown as a ratio / % change, not a simple difference** — FeNO is right-skewed (右偏), so it is log-transformed (对数转换) and reported as the **geometric-mean ratio (几何均值比) to baseline**.
- **More of a biomarker role** — FeNO change from baseline is usually a **secondary / pharmacodynamic (PD, 药效学) endpoint** that confirms the drug suppresses type 2 inflammation; FEV1 and exacerbations (急性加重) are the primary efficacy endpoints.
- **Baseline FeNO also enriches / predicts** — patients with high baseline FeNO respond better to type 2 biologics, so the baseline value is often used to stratify (分层) or enrich (富集) enrollment, not only measured for how much it drops.

Part of the [[Asthma]] therapeutic area; it goes with the lung-function readout [[Pre-BD and Post-BD FEV1]], the blood-based [[Type 2 Inflammation Biomarkers (Asthma)]], and the controller [[Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)]].
