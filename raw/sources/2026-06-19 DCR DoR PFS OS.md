Q: Explain DCR, DoR, and PFS/OS.

Two families. RESPONSE-based (derive from BOR): ORR, DCR, DoR. TIME-TO-EVENT (survival): PFS, OS.

DCR (Disease Control Rate) = proportion of patients whose BOR is CR, PR, OR SD. It adds stable disease to ORR's CR/PR — i.e. "the disease did not progress". Softer/weaker signal than ORR; useful for cytostatic drugs that halt rather than shrink. When SD must last a minimum duration it's often called CBR (Clinical Benefit Rate). SD is ambiguous without a control arm (could be indolent disease, not drug effect), so DCR is interpreted cautiously.

DoR (Duration of Response) = among RESPONDERS only (BOR CR/PR), time from first documented CR/PR until progression or death. Measures how LONG the response lasts = durability. Summarized by Kaplan-Meier median. Pairs with ORR: ORR = whether it responded, DoR = how long.

PFS (Progression-Free Survival) = time from randomization to first PD or death from any cause, whichever comes first; patients with neither are censored. Captures delay of progression. Read out EARLIER than OS, doesn't need to wait for deaths. Caveats: depends on the assessment schedule (interval censoring, evaluation-time bias), needs blinded independent central review (BICR) to limit ascertainment bias, and censoring rules depend on the estimand (subsequent therapy = an intercurrent event). A common surrogate for OS.

OS (Overall Survival) = time from randomization to death from any cause. The GOLD-STANDARD true endpoint — objective, unambiguous, not assessment-dependent. Caveats: needs long follow-up and many death events, large sample, and is confounded/diluted by subsequent therapies and crossover.

Relationship: OS is the true clinical benefit; ORR / DoR / DCR / PFS are earlier readouts, with PFS (validated in some tumours, not all) and response acting as surrogate endpoints for OS. In prostate cancer the radiographic version rPFS is used.
