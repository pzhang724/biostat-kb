Q: 说说 Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT)

A:
PRRT — Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy — a form of radioligand therapy where a
radiolabeled peptide binds a cell-surface receptor overexpressed on tumour cells, delivering
targeted internal radiation from inside the cell.

- Mechanism: a tumour-targeting peptide (ligand) carries a therapeutic radionuclide; the peptide
  docks onto a receptor overexpressed on the cancer cell, so the β⁻ (or α) radiation is deposited
  at/inside that cell. A subtype of RPT — internal, systemic, biology-targeted radiation.
- Archetype: somatostatin analogues (DOTATATE / DOTATOC) labeled with ¹⁷⁷Lu (or ⁹⁰Y), targeting
  somatostatin receptors (SSTR) overexpressed on gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumours
  (GEP-NET).
- Approved drug: ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE (Lutathera), for SSTR-positive GEP-NETs; pivotal trial NETTER-1.
- Theranostic pairing: ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET images/selects patients (same SSTR target); ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE
  treats — diagnostic + therapeutic on one target.
- Relation to PSMA therapy: same theranostic logic as ¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA-617 in prostate cancer. PRRT
  classically denotes the peptide→receptor (SSTR / NET) class; PSMA-617 is a small-molecule ligand.
  Same kidney/marrow dose-limiting concerns as other ¹⁷⁷Lu agents.
