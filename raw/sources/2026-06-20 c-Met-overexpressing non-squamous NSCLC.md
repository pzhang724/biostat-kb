Q: 解释一下 c-Met-overexpressing non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer

A: It is a biomarker-selected lung-cancer trial population, read piece by piece:

NSCLC = Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer — the major class of lung cancer (~85%), as opposed to
SCLC (small cell), which is more aggressive/neuroendocrine and treated differently.

Non-squamous = a histologic subtype split inside NSCLC. NSCLC = squamous cell carcinoma vs
non-squamous (mainly adenocarcinoma, plus large cell). The split is treatment-relevant:
some drugs are indicated only in non-squamous (e.g. pemetrexed, bevacizumab), and most
actionable driver alterations (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, MET) are enriched in adenocarcinoma. So
"non-squamous NSCLC" defines an eligibility population.

c-Met overexpressing = MET is a receptor tyrosine kinase (ligand HGF). "c-Met
overexpression" = high MET protein on the tumor cell, measured by IHC. It is a biomarker
defining a subset for MET-targeted therapy. Distinct from other MET abnormalities: MET
amplification (gene copy gain) and MET exon 14 skipping (mutation) — overexpression is the
protein-level readout. c-Met-overexpressing non-squamous NSCLC is the enrollment biomarker
for MET-directed agents such as the antibody-drug conjugate telisotuzumab vedotin (Teliso-V)
and EGFR-MET bispecifics (e.g. amivantamab) / MET inhibitors.

Putting it together: non-small-cell (not small cell), non-squamous histology
(adenocarcinoma-type), tumors that overexpress the c-Met protein — i.e. the patients a
MET-targeted drug is tested in. (Analogous to how PSMA expression selects patients for
177Lu-PSMA in prostate cancer.)
