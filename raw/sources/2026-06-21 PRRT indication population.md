Q: 这个是什么 — inoperable, progressive, somatostatin receptor-positive (SSTR+),
neuroendocrine tumours of gastroenteric or pancreatic origin

A:
This is the target patient population / indication for PRRT (the ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE / Lutathera
label, NETTER-1 population). It is the disease phrase parsed qualifier by qualifier — each word
is an eligibility gate:

- neuroendocrine tumours (NET): tumours arising from neuroendocrine cells (hormone-secreting cells
  scattered through the body); generally slow-growing.
- of gastroenteric or pancreatic origin (GEP-NET): located in the GI tract or the pancreas — the
  classic PRRT indication.
- somatostatin receptor-positive (SSTR+): the tumour overexpresses somatostatin receptors,
  confirmed by ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET (or older octreotide scan). This is the target the peptide binds —
  no receptor, no PRRT (the drug has nothing to dock onto). The selection/theranostic gate.
- inoperable: cannot be cured/removed by surgery; surgery is the curative option for localized NET,
  so "inoperable" means you've moved to systemic therapy.
- progressive: the disease is documented to be growing/worsening (often after a prior somatostatin
  analogue); progression is what justifies escalating to PRRT rather than watchful waiting.

So altogether: a NET in the gut/pancreas, can't be cut out, is actively growing, and shows the SSTR
target — i.e. exactly the patient PRRT is designed for.
