Human asked: 还有 MRI (explain MRI, the imaging principle — following chemotherapy / PET).

Answer given (content marker):

MRI = Magnetic Resonance Imaging (磁共振成像). Anatomical imaging with the best SOFT-TISSUE contrast, and crucially NO ionizing radiation (uses magnetism + radio waves, unlike X-ray/CT/PET).

How it works (原理), the chain:
1. The body is full of hydrogen protons (氢质子) — mostly in water and fat. Each proton spins like a tiny magnet.
2. A strong magnetic field (强磁场, e.g. 1.5T / 3T) aligns these proton spins.
3. A radiofrequency (RF, 射频) pulse tips the spins out of alignment.
4. When the pulse stops, the protons RELAX back and re-emit RF signal. The rate of relaxation differs by tissue — two main constants T1 and T2 — so different tissues give different signal = CONTRAST.
5. Magnetic field gradients (梯度磁场) encode position, so the signal can be reconstructed into a spatial image.

Why it's used:
- Superb soft-tissue contrast — brain, prostate, liver, bone MARROW, spinal cord; far better than CT for these.
- No radiation → safe to repeat; good for younger patients / serial follow-up.
- Functional variants: DWI (diffusion-weighted, 弥散加权 — cellularity, picks up tumour & marrow mets), DCE (dynamic contrast-enhanced — perfusion). Contrast agent = gadolinium (钆), not iodine.
- Prostate cancer: multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) for local staging; whole-body MRI/DWI for bone-marrow metastases.

Trade-offs vs the others: slower and noisier than CT, sensitive to motion, contraindicated with some metal implants/pacemakers.

Modality contrast (one line each):
- CT — X-ray attenuation; fast, great for bone/lung/anatomy; uses radiation.
- MRI — magnetism + RF; best soft tissue; no radiation; slower.
- PET — positron-emitting tracer; FUNCTIONAL/molecular (what the tissue is DOING); usually fused as PET/CT. See [[PET (Positron Emission Tomography)]].
So: CT/MRI = anatomy (MRI better soft tissue, no radiation), PET = function/biology.
