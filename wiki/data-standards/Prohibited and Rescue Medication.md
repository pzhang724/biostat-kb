# Prohibited and Rescue Medication

Two protocol-defined categories of concomitant medication (合并用药) — the line between them is set by the protocol.

- **Prohibited medication** (禁用药物) — drugs a subject is **not allowed** to take during the trial (or in defined windows) because they'd interfere with the study drug, confound the endpoint, or raise a safety concern (e.g. other analgesics in a pain trial, strong CYP inhibitors/inducers). Taking one is a **protocol deviation** (方案偏离) and may trigger discontinuation or exclusion from a per-protocol analysis.

- **Rescue medication** (补救药物) — a pre-specified, **permitted** treatment a subject may take when the study treatment isn't adequately controlling symptoms — in acute pain (急性疼痛), opioid (阿片类) rescue for breakthrough pain (爆发痛). Its use is allowed but recorded and counted: a classic [[Intercurrent Event]] that confounds the efficacy reading, so post-rescue pain scores are usually imputed via [[Imputation and Carried-Forward Methods (LOCF, WOCF, wWOCF)]], and rescue use itself becomes an endpoint — the [[Opioid-Free Rate]], total consumption, time to first rescue.

Both are captured alongside [[Prior, Concomitant, and Subsequent Treatment]]. Part of the [[Acute Pain]] thread.
