# MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)

**MRI (磁共振成像)** — anatomical imaging with the best **soft-tissue** (软组织) contrast and, importantly, **no ionizing radiation** (电离辐射). It uses magnetism and radio waves, unlike X-ray / CT / [[PET (Positron Emission Tomography)|PET]].

## How it works (原理)

1. The body is full of **hydrogen protons (氢质子)** — mostly in water and fat; each one spins like a tiny magnet.
2. A **strong magnetic field (强磁场,** e.g. 1.5T / 3T, T = tesla 特斯拉) lines up their spins.
3. A **radiofrequency (RF, 射频)** pulse tips the spins out of line.
4. When the pulse stops, the protons **relax** (弛豫) back and send out an RF signal again. The relaxation rate is different in each tissue — the constants **T1 and T2** — so tissues give different signals = **contrast** (对比度).
5. **Gradient fields (梯度磁场)** encode position → rebuild a spatial image.

**Why it's used:**

- Excellent soft-tissue contrast — brain, prostate, liver, **bone marrow** (骨髓), spinal cord (脊髓); better than CT for these.
- No radiation → safe to repeat for serial follow-up (多次随访).
- Functional variants: **DWI (diffusion-weighted imaging, 弥散加权成像** — shows cellularity 细胞密度; picks up tumour and marrow metastases, mets 转移灶), DCE (dynamic contrast-enhanced, 动态增强 — perfusion 灌注). Contrast agent (造影剂) = **gadolinium (钆)**, not iodine (碘).
- Prostate cancer: multiparametric MRI (mpMRI, 多参数磁共振) for local staging (分期); whole-body MRI/DWI for bone-marrow metastases.

**Trade-offs (利弊):** slower and noisier than CT, sensitive to movement, and not allowed (contraindicated, 禁忌) with some metal implants (植入物) / pacemakers (起搏器).

## Modality contrast

- **CT** — X-ray attenuation (衰减); fast, great for bone/lung/anatomy; uses radiation.
- **MRI** — magnetism + RF; best soft tissue; no radiation; slower.
- **PET** — a tracer that gives off positrons; **functional/molecular** (what the tissue is *doing*); usually combined as PET/CT.

So **CT/MRI = anatomy** (MRI: better soft tissue, no radiation), **PET = function/biology**. An imaging modality (成像方式) used in [[Radiological Assessment]] within [[Oncology]].
