Q: What do CIOMS, IRB, and IEC each do?

IRB (Institutional Review Board) and IEC (Independent Ethics Committee) are the SAME function under two names — the independent ethics committee that protects the rights, safety, and well-being of human trial subjects. IRB = US term (FDA 21 CFR 56); IEC = the ICH/EU term (ICH E6 GCP).
- Before start: review and approve the protocol, the informed consent form (ICF), the Investigator's Brochure, recruitment materials, and investigator qualifications; weigh risk-benefit; ensure informed consent is adequate.
- Ongoing: continuing review (at least annually), review of amendments, receipt of safety reports (SUSARs / expedited) and progress reports; can suspend or terminate approval.
- Composition: independent members, including scientific and non-scientific / lay community members.
- A site cannot enroll without ethics approval. Ethics approval (IRB/IEC) and regulatory authorization (the authority, e.g. FDA IND) are TWO separate gates — both required.

CIOMS (Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences) — an international NGO (established under WHO/UNESCO). In drug-safety practice it means two things:
- The CIOMS Form (CIOMS I) — the de-facto international standard paper form for an expedited individual case safety report (ICSR); the human-readable analog of an ICH E2B electronic report, used to transmit SUSARs/SAEs to authorities and ethics committees.
- CIOMS working groups authored foundational pharmacovigilance guidance (CIOMS I-VIII), and CIOMS also publishes international ethical guidelines for health-related research.

So: IRB ≈ IEC = the ethics gate (people/well-being); CIOMS = the body behind the standard expedited safety-report form (and PV/ethics guidance) — it's about HOW a SUSAR/SAE is transmitted, not an oversight committee.
