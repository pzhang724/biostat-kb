Q: 但是这里的 tumour 都是一个 tumour 是吧?(checking whether "tumor" = a single tumor)

A:
- No — usually NOT a single tumor. A patient typically has multiple tumor sites: the primary tumor plus metastatic lesions (and metastatic/advanced disease is exactly the setting where these endpoints apply).
- When I said "the tumor shrank" I meant TOTAL TUMOR BURDEN across all lesions, not one tumor.
- How RECIST handles this: pick a limited set of measurable lesions as "target lesions", measure each, and track the SUM OF DIAMETERS (SoD) over time. Other lesions are "non-target" and assessed qualitatively. Response (CR/PR/SD/PD) is judged on the aggregate, not one lesion.
- This is exactly why PSA is attractive: PSA is a SYSTEMIC (whole-body) marker — it reflects total prostate-cancer cell activity across all sites at once, including bone mets that have no measurable diameter. One blood number ~ aggregate burden, whereas RECIST has to sum up individually measurable lesions.
- Caveat that follows: lesions can behave heterogeneously (some shrink, some grow); a single aggregate (SoD or PSA) can hide a mixed response. That's part of why a falling PSA doesn't guarantee every lesion regressed.
