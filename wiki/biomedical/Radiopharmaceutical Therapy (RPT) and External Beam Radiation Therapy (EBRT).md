# Radiopharmaceutical Therapy (RPT) and External Beam Radiation Therapy (EBRT)

Two ways to deliver **ionizing radiation (电离辐射)** to kill cancer, differing by **where the radiation source sits** and how **local vs systemic** the delivery is.

- **EBRT — External Beam Radiation Therapy (外照射放疗)** — radiation aimed at the tumor from an **external machine** (**linear accelerator / linac, 直线加速器**) outside the body. A **local/regional** treatment of a defined target volume, delivered as a **physical procedure** (SDTM **PR** domain), usually **fractionated (分次)** over many daily sessions. Decades of use gave well-characterized normal-organ absorbed-dose tolerances (**NTCP** models, 正常组织并发症概率).
- **RPT — Radiopharmaceutical Therapy (放射性药物治疗 / 放射配体治疗)** — a **radioactive drug** given **systemically (全身给药)** (usually IV, sometimes oral) that circulates and delivers radiation **from inside the body**, targeted to tumor cells by its ligand/biology (e.g. [[Lutetium-177 (177Lu)|¹⁷⁷Lu]]-PSMA-617). Dosed by **activity (Bq/GBq, 给药活度)**, absorbed dose in **Gy**; emits **α / β / γ**. It is a *drug*, recorded as study/con-med treatment — not a beam procedure.

**Key contrast:** external source + local target (EBRT) vs internal source + systemic distribution (RPT). This is exactly why the [[Oncology Therapeutic Radiopharmaceuticals — Dosage Optimization (FDA Draft Guidance)|FDA RPT dosage-optimization guidance]] warns that **EBRT-derived organ dose limits don't simply transfer to RPTs** — the **dose rate** and the **distribution of radiation** differ.
