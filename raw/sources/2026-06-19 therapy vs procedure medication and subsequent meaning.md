Q1: What does "therapy" mean vs procedure vs medication? Hard to tell apart.

A1: They are not three parallel things — medication and procedure are the two concrete recordable TYPES; therapy is a higher-level umbrella.
- MEDICATION = a DRUG / substance given to the patient (a compound) -> CM. The "thing" is a chemical/biologic.
- PROCEDURE = an ACTION / intervention done to the patient -- surgery, radiotherapy delivery, biopsy, catheter, etc. -> PR. The "thing" is an act on the body.
- THERAPY = NOT a third atom at the same level. It's an UMBRELLA word for a COURSE of treatment aimed at the disease. A therapy is DELIVERED THROUGH medications and/or procedures. "Anti-cancer therapy" = the disease-directed treatment, which can be systemic drugs (CM), radiotherapy/surgery (PR), or a regimen combining them.
- So: medication & procedure = the concrete data atoms (CM, PR); therapy = the course/intent realized through them. e.g. "first-line chemotherapy" is one therapy realized as several drug administrations.

Q2: Does "subsequent / 后续" mean after one dose of the drug, or after the whole treatment ends?

A2: The reference point is the WHOLE STUDY-TREATMENT period, NOT a single dose.
- CONCOMITANT = during the treatment period (on study drug, between first and last dose).
- SUBSEQUENT / post = after the WHOLE treatment ENDS (after the last dose / EOT), in follow-up.
- So a medication taken between two study-drug doses during the treatment period is CONCOMITANT, not subsequent. "Subsequent anti-cancer therapy" = the next line of treatment the patient starts after coming OFF the study drug (e.g. after progression) -- that's what confounds OS.
- The exact boundary (last dose date, or last dose + X days, or treatment-discontinuation date) is defined in the SAP/protocol.
