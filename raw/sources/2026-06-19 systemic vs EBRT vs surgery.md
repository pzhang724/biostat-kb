Q: Difference between anti-cancer systemic therapy vs anti-cancer external beam radiation therapy vs anti-cancer surgery.

A: These are the three MODALITIES of cancer treatment, differing by reach, mechanism, and data type.

- SYSTEMIC therapy = treatment that travels through the WHOLE BODY via the bloodstream, hitting cancer cells anywhere. It's a MEDICATION (drug) -> CM. Includes chemotherapy, hormonal/endocrine therapy (ADT for prostate), targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and radioligand/radiopharmaceutical therapy (e.g. 177Lu-PSMA). Given oral / IV / injection.
- EXTERNAL BEAM RADIATION THERAPY (EBRT) = a PROCEDURE delivering radiation from an EXTERNAL machine (linac) aimed at a specific site. LOCAL/regional (a defined area), not whole-body. -> PR. e.g. radiation to the prostate or palliative RT to a bone met; SBRT.
- SURGERY = a PROCEDURE that physically REMOVES/resects tumour or organ. LOCAL. -> PR. e.g. radical prostatectomy, orchiectomy (surgical castration), metastasectomy.

Distinguishing axes:
- REACH: systemic = whole body (drug in bloodstream); EBRT & surgery = LOCAL (one site).
- MECHANISM: systemic = drug; EBRT = radiation energy from outside; surgery = physical removal.
- DATA TYPE: systemic = medication (CM); EBRT & surgery = procedure (PR).

Key caveat (relevant to the radioligand thread): radioligand therapy (177Lu-PSMA) uses radiation but is SYSTEMIC -- the radioisotope is injected and travels to PSMA+ cells everywhere -- so it's a SYSTEMIC therapy / medication, NOT EBRT. EBRT is the external-beam (local) form of radiation.

Prostate tie-in: castration can be SURGICAL (orchiectomy = surgery) or MEDICAL (ADT = systemic drug) -- same goal, different modality.
