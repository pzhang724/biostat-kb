# Follow-up of Adverse Events

Once an AE is recorded, the investigator must **follow it** until a **final outcome** — resolution / return to baseline, stabilization, death, or until it's adequately explained or the patient is lost to follow-up. Follow-up is the process that moves the [[Adverse Event Outcome|outcome]] RECOVERING → RECOVERED and fills in the end date `AEENDTC` (and the AE duration used in analysis).

## Key points

- **Extends beyond scheduled visits / end of treatment** if the AE is still ongoing — especially [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)|SAEs]] and drug-related AEs. A **related SAE is followed even after** the standard [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#Safety reporting period|safety reporting period]] ends — causality, not the calendar.
- **SAE follow-up reports** — an SAE is reported in stages: an **initial report**, then **follow-up reports** as new information arrives (outcome updates, causality reassessed, extra data). The sponsor sends these onward to authorities — see the [[Serious Adverse Event (SAE)#Reporting flow to the regulatory authority|reporting flow]].
- **Ongoing AEs at end of study** — the protocol specifies following them until resolution / stabilization or a defined cutoff; sometimes care is handed to the patient's regular physician.
- **Practically** — abnormal labs are repeated until they normalize or stabilize, which is what closes out `AEENDTC`.

## Not the "safety follow-up visit"

Distinguish from the scheduled **safety follow-up visit / period** on the [[Schedule of Assessments]] — that is a fixed study timepoint; AE follow-up is **event-level** tracking of one AE that can extend past it. How the AE was first found is [[Adverse Event Ascertainment]]; a cross-cutting safety concept in [[Oncology]].
