Q: Re-explain — why does this kind of (no-measurable-disease / NED) case get included?

A: "Included" happens for two different reasons depending on the trial:

1. Adjuvant trial — inclusion IS the point. The trial deliberately enrolls disease-free (post-curative-surgery) patients because the question being asked is "does the drug prevent recurrence?" They are not in by accident — they ARE the target population. There's no measurable tumour, so the endpoint is not ORR/response but DFS (disease-free survival). NED is simply the label for their ongoing "no disease detected" state through the study.

2. Metastatic / measurable-disease trial where a patient becomes NED mid-study (e.g. surgery removes the disease) — they were validly enrolled (had measurable disease at baseline). Once randomized, INTENTION-TO-TREAT (ITT) says you keep and keep tracking them to the end; you don't delete a patient because their disease was resected. So at that timepoint you record NED rather than dropping them. (Whether they still count toward the strict ORR/response-evaluable set is governed separately by the baseline-measurable-disease rule.)

Core: a patient is included either BY DESIGN (adjuvant = the population the trial is about) or BY ITT (once enrolled you follow everyone, regardless of how their disease evolves). NED is the status that lets you describe a disease-free timepoint without misusing CR.
