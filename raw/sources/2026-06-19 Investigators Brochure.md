Q: What is the Investigator's Brochure (IB) for?

A: The IB (研究者手册) is the compiled body of all clinical + nonclinical data on the investigational product that is relevant to studying it in humans. Defined by ICH E6 (GCP). It's the investigator's single reference on the drug.

Purpose / what it's for:
- Give investigators the information they need to understand and comply with the protocol — the rationale, dose, schedule, procedures.
- Support an informed benefit-risk assessment for running the trial and treating subjects.
- Provide the safety baseline used during the trial.

Typical contents: physicochemical / pharmaceutical info; nonclinical data (pharmacology, toxicology, animal PK); clinical data so far (human PK/PD, safety, any efficacy); and a summary + guidance to the investigator.

Key link to safety reporting: the IB contains the **Reference Safety Information (RSI)** — the list of expected serious adverse reactions. That list is what "expected vs unexpected" is judged against when deciding if an SAR is a SUSAR.

It is a living document — updated periodically (at least annually) as new data accrue. Once the drug is approved, the label / SmPC takes over the reference-safety role.
