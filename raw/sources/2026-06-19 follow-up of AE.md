Q: Follow-up of an AE.

A: Once an AE is recorded, the investigator must FOLLOW IT until a final outcome — resolution / return to baseline, stabilization, death, or until it's adequately explained or the patient is lost to follow-up. Follow-up is the process that moves the outcome RECOVERING -> RECOVERED and fills in the end date AEENDTC (and the AE duration used in analysis).

Key points:
- Follow-up continues BEYOND the patient's scheduled visits / end of treatment if the AE is still ongoing — especially SAEs and drug-related AEs. A related SAE is followed even after the standard safety reporting period ends (causality, not the calendar).
- SAE follow-up reports: an SAE is reported in stages — an initial report, then follow-up reports as new information arrives (outcome updates, causality reassessed, extra data). The sponsor sends these follow-up reports onward to authorities.
- Ongoing AEs at end of study: the protocol specifies following them until resolution / stabilization or a defined cutoff; sometimes care is handed to the patient's regular physician.
- Practically: abnormal labs are repeated until they normalize or stabilize, which is what closes out AEENDTC.

Distinguish from the scheduled "safety follow-up visit / period" on the Schedule of Assessments — that is a fixed study timepoint; AE follow-up is event-level tracking of one AE that can extend past it.
