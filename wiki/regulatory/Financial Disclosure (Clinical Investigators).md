# Financial Disclosure (Clinical Investigators)

An FDA requirement (**21 CFR 54**; CFR = US Code of Federal Regulations, 美国联邦法规) whose job is to let FDA judge possible **bias in the study data** caused by an investigator's **financial interest** (经济利益) in the outcome of the trial. It is about **data integrity / reliability** (数据完整性/可靠性), **not** about subject ethics.

## Who / when

- The **sponsor** collects financial information from each clinical investigator (and sub-investigators, 助理研究者) and submits it **with the marketing application (NDA/BLA**, new drug application / biologics license application, 新药/生物制品上市申请) — covering the study period plus one year after.
- Investigators certify or disclose on **FDA Form 3454** (certifies that there are **no** interests to disclose) or **Form 3455** (discloses the interests).

## What must be disclosed

- **Compensation (报酬) that depends on the outcome** of the study (any amount).
- **Large payments of other kinds** from the sponsor (grants 资助, equipment, retainers 聘用费) above a threshold (**>$25,000** beyond study costs).
- A **proprietary interest** (所有权权益) in the product — patent (专利), trademark (商标), copyright (版权), or licensing agreement (许可协议).
- A **significant equity interest** (重大股权) in the sponsor — **>$50,000** in a publicly traded sponsor (上市公司), or **any** equity in a sponsor that is not publicly traded.

## What FDA does with it

If there are interests to disclose, FDA judges whether they could have **biased the data**, and may require **extra independent analyses or audits** (稽查), or **discount / give less weight to** (down-weight) that site's data.

## Vs the IRB conflict-of-interest check

These are different ways of looking at conflict of interest (COI, 利益冲突): the [[IRB and IEC (Ethics Committees)|IRB/IEC]] looks at COI for **ethics / subject protection while the trial runs**; financial disclosure is a **data-bias** check aimed at the regulator, made **at submission**. A trial-conduct concept used across [[Oncology]].
