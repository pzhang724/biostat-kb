Q: Immunogenicity 这个是什么意思

A: Immunogenicity = a therapeutic's ability to provoke an immune response in the patient — the
body recognizes the drug as foreign and mounts an immune reaction, mainly by producing anti-drug
antibodies (ADA). It is chiefly a concern for biologics (large-molecule products: monoclonal
antibodies, fusion proteins, antibody-drug conjugates, enzymes), and more so for non-human /
chimeric ones; small-molecule drugs are rarely immunogenic.

The immune system makes ADA against the drug; a subset are neutralizing antibodies (NAb) that
block the drug's active site/function.

Why it matters (three axes):
- Efficacy: ADA/NAb neutralize the drug or speed its clearance -> less exposure, lost activity.
- Safety: hypersensitivity / infusion reactions, up to anaphylaxis; rarely ADA cross-react with
  an endogenous protein (serious).
- PK: ADA alter clearance / half-life (exposure changes over time).

How it's assessed: a tiered bioanalytical strategy — screening assay -> confirmatory assay ->
titer -> neutralizing-antibody assay. Reported as ADA incidence/prevalence, titer, timing
(treatment-emergent / treatment-boosted), and persistence. It is a specialty (non-CRF, external
vendor) lab feed. In CDISC it maps to the SDTM IS domain (Immunogenicity Specimen Assessments).

Regulatory: FDA/EMA/ICH have immunogenicity-assessment guidance; required in biologic
development and central to biosimilars (comparative immunogenicity vs the reference product).
