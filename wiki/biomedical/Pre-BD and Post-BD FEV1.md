# Pre-BD and Post-BD FEV1

**FEV1** = forced expiratory volume in 1 second（一秒用力呼气容积），the core lung-function measure from **spirometry**（肺量计）.

- **Pre-BD** = pre-bronchodilator — measured *before* an inhaled bronchodilator.
- **Post-BD** = post-bronchodilator — measured *after* a short-acting β2-agonist (e.g. salbutamol / albuterol).
- The difference is **reversibility（可逆性）**: a post-BD FEV1 improvement of **≥12% and ≥200 mL** indicates reversible airflow limitation — supportive diagnostic evidence for asthma, distinguishing it from the relatively fixed airflow limitation of COPD.

In asthma trials, pre-BD FEV1 is commonly an **inclusion criterion** and a **baseline / efficacy endpoint**; demonstrated reversibility is often an entry requirement.

**Direction**: a higher FEV1 value = better airflow / lung function, so raising baseline pre-BD FEV1 is the treatment goal. But a *larger* Pre→Post reversibility is **not** "better" — it is a diagnostic signal of reversible airway disease, not a treatment target; with good control, baseline FEV1 rises and the need for rescue bronchodilator reversibility falls.

**Change from baseline of pre-BD FEV1** is one of the most common primary / key efficacy endpoints in asthma trials — the pre-rescue FEV1 at a landmark visit (e.g. week 24) minus the baseline value (in mL or L). Pre-BD is used (not post-BD) because it reflects the patient's own **maintained underlying lung function**, not yet lifted by a bronchodilator — so a rise shows the drug **sustainably** improved baseline airway function. The trial reads the **treatment-vs-control difference** in change from baseline, typically via **MMRM** (mixed model for repeated measures) adjusting for baseline FEV1.

Part of the [[Asthma]] therapeutic area; a companion biomarker is [[FeNO (Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide)]].
