Q: 解释一下 RPT, EBRT

A: Both are ways of delivering ionizing radiation to kill cancer, differing by where the
radiation source sits and how targeted/systemic it is.

EBRT = External Beam Radiation Therapy. Radiation is aimed at the tumor from an external
machine (a linear accelerator) outside the body. It is a LOCAL/regional treatment of a
defined target volume, delivered as a physical procedure (recorded in SDTM PR), usually
fractionated over many daily sessions. Decades of EBRT experience gave well-characterized
normal-organ absorbed-dose tolerances (NTCP models).

RPT = Radiopharmaceutical Therapy (a.k.a. oncology therapeutic radiopharmaceutical /
radioligand therapy). A radioactive drug given SYSTEMICALLY (usually IV, sometimes oral)
that circulates and delivers radiation from INSIDE the body, targeted to tumor cells by its
ligand/biology (e.g. 177Lu-PSMA-617). It is a drug (dosed by activity in Bq/GBq, absorbed
dose in Gy; emits alpha/beta/gamma), recorded as study/con-med treatment, not a beam
procedure.

Key contrast: external source + local target (EBRT) vs internal source + systemic
distribution (RPT). This is exactly why the FDA RPT dosage-optimization guidance says
EBRT-derived organ dose limits don't simply transfer to RPTs — the dose rate and the
distribution of radiation differ.
