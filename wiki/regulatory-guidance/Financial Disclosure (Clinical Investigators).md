# Financial Disclosure (Clinical Investigators)

An FDA requirement (**21 CFR 54**) whose job is to let FDA assess potential **bias in the study data** caused by an investigator's **financial interest** (经济利益) in the trial outcome. It is about **data integrity / reliability**, **not** subject ethics.

## Who / when

- The **sponsor** collects financial information from each clinical investigator (and sub-investigators) and submits it **with the marketing application (NDA/BLA)** — covering the study period plus one year after.
- Investigators certify or disclose via **FDA Form 3454** (certification that there are **no** disclosable interests) or **Form 3455** (disclosure of the interests).

## What must be disclosed

- **Compensation affected by the outcome** of the study (any amount).
- **Significant payments of other sorts** from the sponsor (grants, equipment, retainers) above a threshold (**>$25,000** beyond study costs).
- A **proprietary interest** in the product — patent, trademark, copyright, or licensing agreement.
- A **significant equity interest** in the sponsor — **>$50,000** in a publicly traded sponsor, or **any** equity in a non-publicly-traded sponsor.

## What FDA does with it

If disclosable interests exist, FDA evaluates whether they could have **biased the data** and may require **additional independent analyses or audits**, or **discount / down-weight** that site's data.

## Vs the IRB conflict-of-interest check

Different conflict-of-interest lenses: the [[IRB and IEC (Ethics Committees)|IRB/IEC]] looks at COI for **ethics / subject protection during conduct**; financial disclosure is a **regulator-facing, data-bias** check made **at submission**. A cross-cutting trial-conduct concept in [[Oncology]].
