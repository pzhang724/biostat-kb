Human (Acute Pain thread): "说一下 prohibited medication 和 rescue medication"

Answer captured:

- Prohibited medication: drugs a subject is NOT allowed to take during the trial (or during
  defined windows) because they would interfere with the study drug, confound the endpoint, or
  raise a safety concern (e.g. other analgesics in a pain trial, strong CYP inhibitors/inducers).
  Taking one is a protocol deviation and may trigger discontinuation or exclusion from a
  per-protocol analysis.

- Rescue medication: a pre-specified, PERMITTED treatment a subject may take when the study
  treatment isn't adequately controlling symptoms — in acute pain, opioid (or other analgesic)
  rescue for breakthrough pain. Its use is allowed but recorded and counted: it's a classic
  intercurrent event that confounds the efficacy reading, so post-rescue pain scores are usually
  imputed (WOCF/BOCF), and rescue use itself becomes an endpoint (opioid-free rate, total
  consumption, time to first rescue).

Both are concomitant medications, captured alongside prior/concomitant/subsequent treatment.
The line between them is protocol-defined: rescue is the sanctioned escape valve, prohibited is
off-limits.
