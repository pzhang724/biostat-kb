Q: For each subject at each visit, are the lesions to pick all chosen by the investigator at the site? And where is the difference between local vs central assessment?

A:
Lesion selection happens ONCE at BASELINE, by whoever reads the images. At later visits you do NOT re-select — you re-measure the SAME target lesions and look for new lesions. New lesions get added; the target set stays fixed.

But "who reads" can be two parallel readers:
- LOCAL / INVESTIGATOR assessment — the site investigator or local radiologist selects the target lesions at baseline, measures them each visit, and assigns the response. This drives REAL-TIME clinical decisions (treat / hold / stop, next cycle). Fast, but potentially biased — the reader is unblinded and invested in the patient.
- CENTRAL assessment = BICR (Blinded Independent Central Review) — the images are sent to a central imaging core lab where independent radiologists, BLINDED to treatment arm and to the site's read, select THEIR OWN target lesions and derive THEIR OWN response, independent of the site. Usually 2 independent readers + an adjudicator to resolve discordance. Gives an unbiased, standardized efficacy readout.

So: at the site, yes the investigator picks and follows the lesions for the LOCAL read; but if the trial has central review, the central readers pick their OWN lesions independently — the two reads are PARALLEL and can choose different target lesions and reach different responses.

Where the difference lives:
- WHO + WHERE: local = treating investigator at the site; central = independent blinded radiologists at a core lab.
- PURPOSE: local drives patient management in real time; central provides the unbiased endpoint for regulators (especially ORR / PFS primary or key secondary in OPEN-LABEL trials, where investigator assessment of progression timing is bias-prone).
- DATA: TREVAL / RSEVAL = INVESTIGATOR vs INDEPENDENT ASSESSOR distinguishes the two parallel record sets. The SAP says which read drives the primary endpoint; local-vs-central concordance is itself analyzed. Central-read data arrives as a non-CRF external feed.
