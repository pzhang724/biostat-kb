Q: What is Financial Disclosure doing here (clinical trials)?

A: Financial Disclosure by Clinical Investigators is an FDA requirement (21 CFR 54). Its job is to let FDA assess potential BIAS in the study data caused by an investigator's financial interest in the trial outcome. It is about DATA INTEGRITY / reliability, NOT about subject ethics.

Who / when:
- The SPONSOR collects financial information from each clinical investigator (and sub-investigators) and submits it WITH the marketing application (NDA/BLA), covering the period of the study and one year after.
- Investigators certify or disclose via FDA Form 3454 (certification that there are NO disclosable interests) or Form 3455 (disclosure of the interests).

What must be disclosed (the disclosable interests):
- Compensation to the investigator that is AFFECTED BY THE OUTCOME of the study (any amount).
- Significant payments of other sorts from the sponsor (e.g. grants, equipment, retainers) above a threshold (>$25,000 beyond study costs).
- A PROPRIETARY INTEREST in the tested product — patent, trademark, copyright, or licensing agreement.
- A significant EQUITY interest in the sponsor — >$50,000 in a publicly traded sponsor, or ANY equity in a non-publicly-traded sponsor.

What FDA does with it: if disclosable interests exist, FDA evaluates whether they could have biased the data and may require additional independent analyses or audits, or discount/down-weight that site's data.

How it differs from the IRB conflict-of-interest check: the IRB looks at COI for ETHICS / subject protection during conduct; financial disclosure is a REGULATOR-facing, DATA-BIAS check made at submission. Two different conflict-of-interest lenses.
