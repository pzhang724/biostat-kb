# Chemotherapy

**Chemotherapy (化疗)** — the **cytotoxic (细胞毒)** systemic-drug modality of cancer treatment: chemical agents that kill cells by disrupting the machinery of **cell division**.

## Principle (原理)

Cancer's defining trait is uncontrolled rapid proliferation, so **fast-dividing cells are more vulnerable** — chemo exploits that difference. Main classes by the step they hit:

- **Alkylating agents (烷化剂)** — cross-link / damage DNA so it can't replicate.
- **Antimetabolites (抗代谢药)** — fake building blocks that block DNA/RNA synthesis (e.g. methotrexate, 5-FU).
- **Anti-microtubule / spindle poisons (微管抑制剂)** — block the mitotic spindle so the cell can't split (**taxanes** e.g. docetaxel; vinca alkaloids).
- **Topoisomerase inhibitors (拓扑异构酶抑制剂)** — jam the DNA-unwinding enzymes.
- **Anti-tumour antibiotics (抗肿瘤抗生素)** — e.g. anthracyclines (DNA intercalation + free radicals).

Net effect: damage DNA / stop mitosis → trigger apoptosis. It is **systemic** (travels everywhere via blood), unlike local surgery / radiotherapy.

**Why the toxicity:** chemo is **not cancer-specific** — it hits any fast-renewing tissue, so the classic side effects are bone marrow (cytopenias), GI lining (mucositis/nausea), hair follicles (alopecia). That non-selectivity is the core limitation, and the reason targeted/immune therapies were developed.

## Why it is called "therapy" (为什么是 therapy)

- **"-therapy" (疗法 / 治疗)** = a **treatment modality** given for clinical benefit — same suffix as radio*therapy*, immuno*therapy*, hormone *therapy*. It denotes "a way of treating disease," not a specific drug.
- **"Chemo-therapy"** literally = "treatment by **chemical** agents." The term comes from **Paul Ehrlich** (early 1900s), for using chemicals to selectively kill the disease agent.
- So it is "therapy" because it is one of the **modalities** of cancer treatment — the systemic-cytotoxic one — alongside surgery (手术), radiotherapy (放疗), and newer systemic modalities: targeted therapy, immunotherapy, hormone/endocrine therapy, and **radioligand therapy** ([[Lutetium-177 (177Lu)]]). The name marks it as a treatment *approach* defined by mechanism (chemical/cytotoxic), not by drug name.
