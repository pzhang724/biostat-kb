From chat, 2026-06-19 (follow-up to tumour response ordering)

Q: Why does the NED category exist at all (separate from CR)?

A: Because some patients have NO measurable disease to "respond", so CR doesn't apply — you need a different label.

- CR means measurable disease that you HAD has disappeared — i.e. a response to treatment. If a patient had no measurable/evaluable lesions to begin with, there's nothing to shrink to zero, so "CR" is logically impossible; their state is "still no detectable disease" = NED.
- Main setting: ADJUVANT trials — after curative surgery/resection the patient is disease-free (NED) but at recurrence risk; the drug is given to prevent recurrence. There's no measurable target, so no ORR/CR; the endpoint is DFS (disease-free survival), where baseline = NED and the event is recurrence.
- Surgical conversion: a patient can be rendered NED by surgery / local therapy (e.g. resecting metastases). That disease-free state is NOT a drug-induced imaging response, so labeling it CR would wrongly credit the drug. NED keeps it distinct.
- Net effect: NED separates "no disease to assess / disease-free achieved by other means" from genuine treatment response (CR/PR). That keeps ORR clean — counting surgically-achieved NED as a response would inflate/confound the response rate.

So NED exists to cover the no-baseline-disease / disease-free-by-other-means cases that CR can't legitimately describe.
