From chat, 2026-06-19. Follow-up to the progression-capture-split question.

Human asked: 为什么来两次 (why two scans / why does the patient come twice for progression?).

Answer given (content marker):

Because ONE scan cannot tell true progression apart from a fake-out. A single snapshot showing "more/new lesions" has three innocent explanations:
- Bone-scan flare (闪烁现象): when treatment is working, healing bone osteoblasts light up MORE on the bone scan early on — looks like new lesions but is actually response.
- Immunotherapy pseudoprogression (假性进展): immune-cell infiltration transiently swells a lesion / makes new ones appear before it shrinks.
- Measurement noise: slice position, reader judgment, a 20%-borderline call.

A single point in time can't distinguish "growing because the cancer is winning" from "looks bigger but will settle/shrink." The only way to tell is the DIRECTION OF CHANGE OVER TIME: scan again after an interval —
- if it KEEPS getting worse (≥2 more new lesions / further RECIST growth) → real progression, confirmed.
- if it stabilizes or improves → it was flare/pseudoprogression, NOT progression; keep treating.

So the second visit is the control that filters out the transient phenomena. It's exactly why the second "2" exists in PCWG3's 2+2 rule and why iRECIST needs iUPD→iCPD. Cost of getting it wrong is high: a false PD pulls a patient off a therapy that is actually working. The delay between the two scans is essential — too soon and the flare hasn't resolved yet.

One line: 一次扫描分不清"真长大"还是"flare/假进展/噪声";只有隔一段时间再扫、看变化方向,才能确认 —— 继续恶化=真进展,稳定/好转=虚惊。
