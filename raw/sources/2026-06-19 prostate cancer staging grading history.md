Q: Prostate cancer history concepts — histologic/cytologic, clinical TNM, disease stage, Gleason score, radical prostatectomy pathological TNM, castration.

HISTOLOGIC vs CYTOLOGIC (how the cancer was confirmed)
- Histologic = diagnosis from TISSUE architecture (core needle biopsy / specimen under microscope showing gland structure). Prostate cancer is almost always histologically confirmed adenocarcinoma.
- Cytologic = diagnosis from individual CELLS (e.g. fine-needle aspiration / fluid cytology), no tissue architecture. Less common for prostate.

TNM (the staging framework): T = primary Tumour extent, N = regional Nodes, M = distant Metastasis.
- T: T1 not palpable/incidental, T2 confined to prostate, T3 beyond capsule / seminal vesicle, T4 invades adjacent structures.
- N: N0 none, N1 regional node mets. M: M0 none, M1 distant (M1a non-regional nodes, M1b bone, M1c other).

CLINICAL TNM (cTNM) = assessed BEFORE definitive treatment from exam (DRE), imaging, biopsy. Pre-treatment clinical estimate.

DISEASE STAGE = overall stage group (I-IV) derived from TNM + PSA + grade group. Stage IV = M1 = metastatic (the mCRPC setting).

GLEASON SCORE = histologic GRADE of prostate adenocarcinoma by glandular architecture. Pathologist gives the two most common patterns (each 1-5); sum = score (practically 6-10), e.g. 3+4=7; higher = more poorly differentiated / aggressive. Modern equivalent = ISUP Grade Group 1-5 (GG1 = <=6, GG2 = 3+4, GG3 = 4+3, GG4 = 8, GG5 = 9-10).

RADICAL PROSTATECTOMY = surgical removal of the whole prostate (+ seminal vesicles).
PATHOLOGICAL TNM (pTNM) = staging from the EXAMINED SURGICAL SPECIMEN after prostatectomy (margins, extracapsular extension, seminal vesicle, nodes). More accurate than cTNM. No pT1 for prostate (needs the specimen) -> pT2/pT3/pT4. So cTNM (before, clinical) vs pTNM (after surgery, pathological) = same framework, different time-point + accuracy.

CASTRATION = lowering testosterone to "castrate" level (typically <50 ng/dL). Prostate cancer is androgen-driven, so this slows it. Surgical castration = orchiectomy; medical castration = ADT (LHRH agonist/antagonist). Castration-sensitive vs castration-RESISTANT (CRPC = progresses despite castrate testosterone); mCRPC = metastatic CRPC.
